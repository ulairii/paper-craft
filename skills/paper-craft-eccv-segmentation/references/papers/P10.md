## Writing move: Define the interface before selling a foundation-model combination

Open-Vocabulary SAM defines its product as interactive segmentation and recognition from points or boxes. SAM2CLIP transfers segmentation features into adapters on a frozen CLIP encoder; CLIP2SAM supplies region recognition. The two transfers explain why merely connecting two pretrained models is insufficient.

## Experimental logic

Ground-truth prompts first isolate mask and recognition behavior. Detector-generated prompts then test a less ideal input, while fixed detector labels isolate mask quality. Module and multi-scale comparisons explain the architecture. This ordering lets each experiment answer a different question about the combined system.

## Detail placement

Show which encoder disappears at inference and which weights stay frozen. Prompt type and source belong beside the result because they change the task. The appendix explains that classification and detection datasets train the recognition head without mask supervision; keep that distinction when describing vocabulary scaling.

## Interpretation

A vocabulary containing over twenty thousand labels is not evidence of uniformly reliable segmentation for every label. The large-vocabulary setting lacks the same baseline comparison as the core benchmark. Supplementary LVIS results are comparable or slightly worse than SAM for mask quality, and failures include fine-grained labels and occlusion. These observations sharpen the contribution: useful joint interaction and recognition with reduced computation.
