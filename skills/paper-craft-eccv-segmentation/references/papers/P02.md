## Writing move: Divide computation by the information each path must preserve

BiSeNet makes the accuracy–speed problem concrete: aggressive downsampling loses detail, whereas preserving resolution throughout is expensive. A shallow spatial path and a rapidly downsampled context path each have a distinct job. Explain this division before presenting the fusion and attention modules.

## Experimental logic

The experiments progress from context-path design to adding spatial features and learned fusion. Global pooling and attention refinement are also tested separately and together. The combined gain is not simply the sum of isolated gains, which makes interaction between complementary information a better interpretation than a list of effective blocks.

## Detail placement

Specify what resolution each path keeps and what the fusion combines. Preserve input size next to speed: the Cityscapes speed discussion resizes images to 1536 × 768, although source images are 2048 × 1024. The highest-accuracy backbone and fastest model are different configurations.

## Interpretation

A two-path design balances retained detail and context; it does not preserve every spatial detail. A larger receptive field does not guarantee improvement on every dataset. Present the demonstrated operating point rather than combining the best accuracy and best FPS from different rows.
