## Writing move: Let the annotation structure organize the architecture

UPerNet starts with a broader task: understanding scenes, objects, parts, materials, and textures together. Heterogeneous annotations are therefore part of the problem, not an inconvenience hidden in implementation. Matching prediction heads to feature levels follows from the granularity of each target.

## Experimental logic

The FPN and pooling comparisons establish the shared representation before multi-task results ask which tasks benefit from joint learning. Table 3 includes decreases for object and part segmentation alongside better material recognition. The useful lesson is to explain the tradeoff, not to claim universal positive transfer.

## Detail placement

Keep label-space construction, missing-label treatment, and task-specific supervision in the main account. Texture training is a later stage with a frozen backbone, so avoid implying that every task is jointly optimized throughout. Exact class-merging lists can live in supporting material.

## Interpretation

Predicted object–material relationships illustrate what the model enables. They are co-occurrence statistics from predictions, not independently established commonsense facts. Occlusion and label ontology affect those relationships. A training-time comparison describes the evaluated systems and recipes, not an inherent speed ratio for all tasks.
