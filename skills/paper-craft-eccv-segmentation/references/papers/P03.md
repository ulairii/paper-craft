## Writing move: Explain an attention design as a direction of information flow

PSANet distinguishes collecting context for a pixel from distributing that pixel's information to other locations. This provides a readable account of two attention branches. Its generic relation formulation introduces the design, but the subsequent decomposition is an approximation, not an exact identity that requires the chosen architecture.

## Experimental logic

Comparisons with pooling, ASPP, and non-local context locate the contribution among plausible alternatives. Compact masks test the relative-position design; single-branch results test whether both directions are needed. A nearly unchanged score on one benchmark should be described as such, rather than sold as a large gain from every branch.

## Detail placement

Explain relative-position indexing and how valid locations are selected from the attention mask. Keep local-feature concatenation in the overview because the output combines local and contextual information. Routine convolution widths can follow later.

## Interpretation

Attention examples illustrate where information is aggregated, not a proof that the network understands semantic relationships. Distinguish stronger training sets from architectural gains. The combined branches can improve mIoU while another metric changes little or decreases.
