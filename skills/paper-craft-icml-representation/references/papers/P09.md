Fang, Alex; Ilharco, Gabriel; Wortsman, Mitchell; Wan, Yuhao; Shankar, Vaishaal; Dave, Achal; Schmidt, Ludwig. **ICML 2022**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fang22a.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/fang22a/fang22a.pdf).

## Writing move: Organize an empirical explanation around competing causes

Data Determines asks which ingredients explain CLIP's robustness. The introduction enumerates plausible causes, and the experiments revisit them in a readable sequence. ImageNet-Captions is an experimental instrument: captions and labels for the same images make an otherwise entangled question easier to study.

## Experimental logic

Effective robustness compares shifted performance with what in-distribution accuracy predicts. This prevents a higher overall accuracy from automatically becoming a robustness explanation. Language initialization, templates, alternative training objectives, and changes of data source probe competing accounts. A minimal-language pipeline on YFCC tests whether rich language supervision is required in that setting.

## Detail placement

Explain the effective-robustness quantity and the information used by each training pipeline. The minimal-language experiment still extracts labels from captions and uses image-only pretraining; it is not wholly language-free. Detailed filtering and training recipes can stay in the appendix once those scientific conditions are clear.

## Interpretation

The result supports an important role for the pretraining distribution within the tested comparisons, not a proof that language never matters. Appendix H leaves the source of MAE's additional robustness open. Appendix J shows that class balancing and early stopping materially affect the minimal-language baseline. A causal story becomes stronger when the alternatives and remaining uncertainties are specific.

## Original writing illustration

Before: We prove language is irrelevant and data alone solves robustness.

After: On the controlled image collection, adding language supervision does not reproduce CLIP's effective robustness. Changing the pretraining distribution does, including with a pipeline that uses only simple caption-derived labels.
