## Writing move: Sell a scientific picture through a prediction ladder

On the Stepwise Nature of Self-Supervised Learning contributes a picture of how representations form. It solves a minimal model, extracts an observable prediction, and then looks for that behavior in increasingly realistic networks. A paper can be compelling because it explains a phenomenon; a new state-of-the-art method is not required.

## Experimental logic

Linear simulations test quantitative trajectories. A wide nonlinear model connects the analysis to networks. ResNet experiments first use small initialization to expose the phenomenon, then standard settings to test its persistence. Hidden-representation measurements ask whether the observation extends beyond the final embeddings described by the theory. Each stage extends the previous one without pretending they provide identical evidence.

## Detail placement

Explain the simplified Barlow Twins loss and initialization conditions before presenting the solution. ## Interpretation

The paper explicitly labels its generic-initialization argument a Result with an informal derivation. Deep-network agreement is qualitative; the initial kernel does not accurately predict all trajectories. Standard settings can grow several directions together, and BYOL did not immediately show the effect. Appendix G proposes speedups for future study rather than demonstrating them. These boundaries preserve the explanatory contribution.
