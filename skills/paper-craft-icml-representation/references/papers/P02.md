# P02: Data-Efficient Image Recognition with Contrastive Predictive Coding

Hénaff, Olivier J.; Srinivas, Aravind; De Fauw, Jeffrey; Razavi, Ali; Doersch, Carl; Eslami, S. M. Ali; van den Oord, Aaron. **ICML 2020**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/henaff20a.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/henaff20a/henaff20a.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 2 and 4.1–4.3; Figures 1–3; Tables 1–4; supplementary Sections A–C.

## Writing move: Sell data efficiency through the comparison the reader cares about

CPCv2 organizes its case around how much labeled data is needed to reach a useful accuracy. This gives the opening a concrete purpose. Improvements to the pretraining recipe make the method a credible representative before the paper tests its central label-efficiency claim and extends the argument to detection and segmentation.

## Experimental logic

Read the label-efficiency curve horizontally as well as vertically: reaching a target accuracy with fewer labels can be more informative than a gain at one label fraction. The encoder still sees the full unlabeled image collection. The downstream nonlinear classifier and fine-tuning are separate contributions to the outcome. Sequential changes to capacity, normalization, prediction directions, and augmentation should not be narrated as independent additive effects.

## Detail placement

State which images are available without labels, how labels are subsampled, and what downstream head is trained. These define the resource saving. Keep detailed schedules separate. The paper also discusses the different model classes in its large-model comparison; this is useful context for the headline, not a reason to bury it.

## Interpretation

The low-label result demonstrates a useful training regime, not human-like learning or a claim that unlabeled data is free. Fixed-feature and fine-tuned results reveal how much adaptation adds. The introduction can confidently emphasize reduced annotation requirements while leaving the capacity and pretraining conditions visible.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our model achieves human-like learning from almost no data.

After: Pretraining on unlabeled images reduces the number of class labels needed to reach the target accuracy. We evaluate this benefit across label fractions, then test whether the learned features also support dense prediction.

These are original illustrations, not quotations or complete descriptions of the source method.
