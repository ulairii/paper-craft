# P03: Understanding Contrastive Representation Learning through Alignment and Uniformity on the Hypersphere

Wang, Tongzhou; Isola, Phillip. **ICML 2020**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20k.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20k/wang20k.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 3–5; Theorem 1; Figures 6–8; Tables 1–2; supplement Sections S1–S2.

## Writing move: Turn an explanatory gap into measurable properties

Alignment and Uniformity starts from a gap between an information-theoretic account and observed representation quality. It replaces a vague explanation with two geometric properties that readers can define, measure, and manipulate. The narrative proceeds from conditional analysis to diagnostics and then to direct optimization of those diagnostics.

## Experimental logic

Comparing many encoders shows an association between the proposed measures and downstream accuracy. Experiments optimizing one measure or both move beyond that association and ask whether the geometry is useful as a training objective. The two stages answer different questions; neither needs to be presented as universal causal proof.

## Detail placement

Normalization, the positive-pair distribution, and the large-negative-sample limit belong near the theoretical statement. Long derivations belong in the supplement. Explain alignment and uniformity before showing their equations, and distinguish a shared minimizer from equality of two objective functions.

## Interpretation

Uniformity alone does not establish useful semantics, and the two goals need not be perfectly achievable together on a finite augmented dataset. Table 2 includes text tasks where the joint objective is worse than the contrastive baseline. This makes the transferable lesson conditional: the geometry is an informative lens and a viable objective, not a guarantee of improvement on every task.
