# P01: A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations

Chen, Ting; Kornblith, Simon; Norouzi, Mohammad; Hinton, Geoffrey. **ICML 2020**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/chen20j.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/chen20j/chen20j.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 2–6; Figures 5, 8–9; Tables 3, 5–6, 8; supplement Sections B.2–B.4, B.8–B.10 and C.

## Writing move: Make a composition of known parts into an explanatory contribution

SimCLR makes its contribution legible through findings about augmentation, projection, and training scale. The reader first sees a small framework, then learns why its parts work together. A study of established components can be a strong paper when it changes how researchers construct the learning problem; it need not pretend that every operation is new.

## Experimental logic

The augmentation comparisons investigate which prediction tasks produce useful features. The asymmetric augmentation study isolates transformations on one branch; it is not identical to the final two-view recipe. The projection-head experiments ask what information the representation retains, rather than merely showing another accuracy increment. Training-duration comparisons explain why a large-batch advantage can shrink with more training.

## Detail placement

Keep the distinction between the encoder representation and the discarded projection output in the main text. It changes both the explanation and the downstream protocol. Put the complete augmentation and optimization recipes in setup or supporting material, after explaining their purpose.

## Interpretation

Contrastive task accuracy is not downstream representation quality. The paper's transformation-prediction experiments offer more specific evidence about retained information than a visualization alone. Describe capacity in headline comparisons: the wide model's result and the standard ResNet-50 result do not support the same matched-model claim.
