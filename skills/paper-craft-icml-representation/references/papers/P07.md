## Writing move: Make scale explain why a simpler data pipeline becomes viable

ALIGN frames careful image–text curation as a scaling bottleneck. Its story connects a simple dual encoder to a large noisy collection, then tests whether scale can compensate for weaker data quality. The practical consequence is a representation that supports retrieval and recognition through the same embedding interface.

## Experimental logic

Table 10 makes the quality–scale argument concrete: at the same small dataset size, noisier data performs worse, while a larger noisy collection can surpass the smaller clean one. Model-size and data-size comparisons explain why capacity matters to that tradeoff. Keep these interactions in the explanation instead of reducing the study to a single biggest-model score.

## Detail placement

Minimal filtering still includes specific image and text filters. Describe what is removed and what supervision remains without listing every cleaning threshold in the introduction. Distinguish zero-shot classification, frozen-feature evaluation, and fine-tuning; they measure different uses of the representation.

## Interpretation

Selected compositional retrieval examples illustrate an interface, not a comprehensive compositional-generalization benchmark. Cross-modal results are stronger than some within-modality semantic results, which fits the training objective. The paper's scale argument does not establish that noise is intrinsically beneficial or that all models keep improving at the same rate.
