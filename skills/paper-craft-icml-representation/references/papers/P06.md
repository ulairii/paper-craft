## Writing move: Make a new task interface the center of the story

CLIP frames natural language as a way to specify visual recognition tasks. The central move is not simply replacing labels with captions: the learned text encoder can construct classifiers for new task descriptions. The paper separates this zero-shot task transfer from representation quality measured with a fitted linear classifier.

## Experimental logic

The objective comparison explains why matching image–text pairs is a practical route to scaling. Broad downstream evaluation then tests the interface, including domains where it struggles. Prompt engineering and ensembling explain how language descriptions become usable classifiers. The zero-shot, few-shot, and linear-probe comparisons answer different questions and deserve different result paragraphs.

## Detail placement

Keep the training supervision, prompt construction, and downstream adaptation protocol near the corresponding result. Zero-shot means transfer without fitting on the target dataset's training labels; it does not guarantee unseen concepts or an absence of human-produced supervision. Put the long prompt inventory and dataset-specific recipes in supporting material.

## Interpretation

Counting, spatial reasoning, and some specialized domains expose meaningful boundaries. A compute axis measuring forward image operations is not a training-cost axis. The original robustness comparisons motivate a question about data and objective, but do not independently isolate all those causes; the later Data Determines paper in this corpus shows how to build that investigation.
