## Writing move: Sell an operating range, not a single winning score

The first figure establishes a family of accuracy–compute tradeoffs. Two challenges organize the method: efficient multiscale fusion and scaling the detector across resource budgets. The reader can therefore see why both a fusion module and a scaling rule belong in the same paper.

## Experimental logic

The experiment section separately explains backbone and fusion gains, compares fusion structures, and then compares scaling dimensions. Curves across budgets answer the opening question better than a single largest-model score. Separate latency and operation-count plots make the practical resource story more concrete.

## Detail placement

One worked fusion equation can explain the repeated operation without listing every node equation. Keep the scaling choices and resource axes visible because they are central to this contribution. Routine implementation details need not displace the explanation of why different detector dimensions scale together.

## Interpretation

The scaling rule is presented as a useful heuristic rather than a global optimum. The segmentation extension illustrates reuse while remaining secondary. Do not make extensive runtime accounting a universal writing requirement: it earns space here because efficiency is the paper’s subject.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our highly efficient detector yields superior performance across the board.
