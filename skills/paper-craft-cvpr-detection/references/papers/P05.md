Cai, Zhaowei and Vasconcelos, Nuno. **CVPR 2018**. [Official paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Cai_Cascade_R-CNN_Delving_CVPR_2018_paper.html) · [Full text](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Cai_Cascade_R-CNN_Delving_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf).

## Writing move: Lead with the failure of the obvious solution

Tighter localization appears to call for a higher training IoU threshold, yet simply raising that threshold hurts. The paper resolves this paradox through two causes: too few positive samples and a mismatch between proposal quality and detector quality. The cascade follows as a way to improve proposals before asking the next detector to apply a stricter criterion.

## Experimental logic

Comparisons with repeated regression and multiple output heads explain why adding stages alone is insufficient. Stage and threshold experiments return to the quality mismatch. High-IoU results matter here because high quality was defined geometrically at the beginning, rather than used as an adjective for overall excellence.

## Detail placement

Keep the changing positive-sample distribution and the role of each stage in the main explanation. Distinguish a diagnostic use of ground-truth boxes from the deployed detector in the sentence introducing that experiment. Put exhaustive backbone configurations behind the central threshold-and-stage argument.

## Interpretation

More stages can favor stricter localization metrics without improving aggregate AP. Explain that tradeoff instead of forcing a monotonic improvement story. The transferable writing move is paradox, diagnosis, intervention, and return to the original failure.

## Original writing illustration

Before: We propose a high-quality multi-stage detection framework.

After: Raising the training threshold leaves too few positive proposals. We instead refine proposals between stages, allowing later detectors to learn from examples that meet stricter localization criteria.
