## Writing move: Replace an inherited distinction with a more useful one

The paper initially asks what separates anchor-based and anchor-free detection. It then narrows the apparent difference to operational choices: anchor count, positive assignment, and regression reference. The early comparison changes the reader’s question from which representation is better to how training positives should be selected.

## Experimental logic

The assignment-by-representation comparison comes before the method because the diagnosis is the contribution’s foundation. Adaptive selection follows from that finding. Later anchor-size, anchor-count, and selection experiments test the implications of the new distinction rather than merely enumerating removable modules.

## Detail placement

Explain why center neighbors are selected and how their overlap statistics set the threshold before presenting the algorithm. Keep the geometric intuition close to the rule. Additional parameter sweeps belong after the conceptual comparison, not at the start of the experiment section.

## Interpretation

The argument concerns the compared one-stage, center-based formulations; it does not collapse every detection family into one. Describe what the experiment changes in our understanding. Training-time assignment also should not be described as a new inference operation.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our adaptive strategy effectively bridges the fundamental paradigm gap.

After: In these detectors, positive-sample assignment explains the performance difference better than whether the regression starts from a box or a point.
