Redmon, Joseph and Farhadi, Ali. **CVPR 2017**. [Official paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/html/Redmon_YOLO9000_Better_Faster_CVPR_2017_paper.html) · [Full text](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017/papers/Redmon_YOLO9000_Better_Faster_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf).

## Writing move: Organize many improvements around reader goals

The Better, Faster, Stronger organization gives a collection of design changes three intelligible purposes. The paper distinguishes the improved standard detector from the much larger vocabulary system. A concrete dog-versus-terrier example makes the label hierarchy necessary before introducing its probabilities.

## Experimental logic

The cumulative improvement table explains the development of the detector. An anchor modification can initially lower precision while improving recall; the prose explains why it remains useful. Resolution sweeps show a family of speed–accuracy choices. Weakly supervised category results then address the separate vocabulary-expansion claim.

## Detail placement

Keep label compatibility and joint-training logic in the main text: readers cannot understand the broader label space without them. Use a compact table for incremental changes instead of giving each a disconnected novelty pitch. Keep the standard and expanded-vocabulary model names distinct.

## Interpretation

The best and worst categories explain the boundary of transferred objectness: animals benefit more than unfamiliar clothing categories. Describe this pattern as a clue about the learned representation. A large supported vocabulary is not equivalent to a fully supervised evaluation of every category.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Numerous sophisticated modules comprehensively enhance our system.

After: We improve localization, reduce computation, and extend the label vocabulary; each change addresses a different limitation of the original detector.
