## Writing move: Make a convergence claim visible from the first figure

The paper moves from slow convergence to a concrete source of difficulty: a query can match different objects across epochs. Denoising provides an easier auxiliary target that bypasses this ambiguity. The narrative separates the training intervention from the inherited detector architecture.

## Experimental logic

The matching-instability analysis supports the diagnosis; learning curves and short-schedule results answer the acceleration claim. Box noise, label noise, and masking experiments explain the training task. The attention-mask result matters to the method’s logic because reconstructing noisy ground truth requires controlling which queries can see it.

## Detail placement

Keep the two query groups, their losses, and removal of denoising queries at inference in the main explanation. The supplementary acceleration and timing tables refine the practical claim. Preliminary known-object and known-label tasks remain in the supplement so they do not compete with convergence as the central story.

## Interpretation

The supplement distinguishes halving training epochs from measured wall-clock savings: the reported per-epoch times differ. Write faster convergence when discussing epochs and actual training time when discussing minutes. Matching instability is the proposed explanatory mechanism, not proof that all other causes disappear.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our training strategy dramatically accelerates detection with no cost.

After: Denoising gives the decoder an auxiliary reconstruction target during training, improving early detection accuracy while leaving the inference procedure unchanged.
