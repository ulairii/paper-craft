# General writing guidance

Use this when the catalog has no suitable specialist. It provides a writing approach, not a claim of conference-specific coverage.

## Find the paper's center

Identify the insight that connects the problem, approach, and most informative result. A methodological paper may explain a new decision or mechanism; an analytical paper may explain a pattern, distinction, or choice. Prefer the story the actual work supports to a generic “novel framework” narrative.

Introduce the concrete problem, explain the unresolved tension, and show how the insight leads to the approach. Give each contribution a distinct job. An idea, a finding, and a resource can be separate contributions; three descriptions of the same method are not.

## Teach the approach

Explain the intuition before the notation. Introduce each component by its role and connect it to a problem the reader already understands. Use a short example when it makes a dependency visible. Follow conceptual dependencies rather than implementation order.

## Explain the results

Organize experiments by what they teach: whether the idea helps, what behavior changes, and where the change matters. Select the comparison that makes each point, then explain its implication. Let tables hold the remaining numbers. Mention conditions where they clarify the result rather than adding a checklist after every paragraph.

## Write plain academic English

Use common, precise words and concrete subjects and verbs. Keep technical terms consistent. Give each paragraph one main purpose and connect paragraphs through reasons rather than decorative transitions. Remove inflated adjectives and redundant sentences while keeping the author's supported confidence.

## Complete the requested draft

Write from the supplied facts. Use concise placeholders for missing results or references and continue with the supported material. Do not fabricate novelty, measurements, or citations. Follow the user's format and scope; a full-draft request should receive prose across the manuscript, not merely headings or an experiment plan.
