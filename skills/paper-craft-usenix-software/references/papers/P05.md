# P05: GRIMOIRE: Synthesizing Structure while Fuzzing

Tim Blazytko; Cornelius Aschermann; Moritz Schlögel; Ali Abbasi; Sergej Schumilo; Simon Wörner; Thorsten Holz. **USENIX Security 2019**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/blazytko) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-blazytko.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–5; grammar inference and mutation examples; structured-input comparisons; appendix.

## Writing move: Sell a lower-information method through its useful complementarity

GRIMOIRE explains why local byte constraints do not capture an input’s broader structure. Its island-based intuition gives coarse grammar inference an accessible meaning before the mutation operations appear. The method learns enough structure to recombine useful fragments without requiring a hand-written grammar. It credits inherited local-constraint handling instead of claiming to infer the complete language.

## Experimental logic

Comparisons with ordinary fuzzers establish the benefit of structure-aware mutations. Comparisons with grammar-based fuzzing test the tradeoff in prior knowledge. A split-budget Nautilus–GRIMOIRE experiment shows complementarity even where Nautilus alone is stronger. The paper can therefore make a useful automation argument without requiring grammar inference to replace expert grammars.

## Detail placement

Keep the generalization example, retained fragments, recombination operation, and source of keywords in the main text. State shared dictionaries and corpus conditions where they affect comparisons. Full grammar fragments, mutation inventories, and program lists fit in supporting material. Explain inherited REDQUEEN functionality as part of the actual system.

## Interpretation

Approximate structure is not a recovered input specification. The evaluated approach does not enforce every variable or constructor dependency, and binary-transfer limits restrict some targets. Input-generation rates alone do not isolate the contribution of a mutation operator. Distinguish the newly found bugs from table entries previously found by others.
