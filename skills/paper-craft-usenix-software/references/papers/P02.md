# P02: MOPT: Optimized Mutation Scheduling for Fuzzers

Chenyang Lyu; Shouling Ji; Chao Zhang; Yuwei Li; Wei-Han Lee; Yu Song; Raheem Beyah. **USENIX Security 2019**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/lyu) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19-lyu.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–5; mutation-distribution motivation, long-run and repeated-run evaluations, Table 8.

## Writing move: Make an overlooked allocation decision into the research question

MOPT asks how the fuzzer should distribute effort across mutation operators. Differences across programs and search stages motivate an adaptive scheduler before particle swarm optimization is introduced. This sequence gives the optimizer a job rather than making a familiar optimization technique the entire novelty claim. The pacemaker mode is another consequential change and deserves its own explanation.

## Experimental logic

The paper combines long searches with shorter repeated trials and a factorial comparison of scheduling and pacemaker changes. This makes it possible to discuss adaptation separately from bypassing expensive deterministic work. Empty-seed experiments identify a useful boundary: allocating mutation effort cannot by itself supply a missing structured starting input.

## Detail placement

Keep the mutation-success signal, adaptation cycle, and pacemaker trigger in the main method. Distinguish a union of bugs across variants from the output of a single configuration. Put full optimizer constants and per-program inventories later. Operator counts from different stages should not be presented as a clean isolated efficiency comparison.

## Interpretation

The adaptive distribution is a useful search heuristic, not a guarantee of the globally optimal schedule. Executions per second can change because the input mix changes. The 97 newly reported vulnerabilities include 66 assigned CVEs and 31 others; the number aggregates variants. Explain why a structured-input program may still benefit from deterministic work instead of declaring one schedule universally best.
