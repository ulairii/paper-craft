## Writing move: Make the research questions match the decisions the method changes

GREYONE organizes its introduction around obtaining dependencies, choosing mutations, and directing evolution. The method follows the same structure. Single-byte perturbations and observed variable changes make inferred dependencies tangible; the subsequent byte, branch, and seed decisions explain how that information becomes useful. The reader can follow the contribution without first learning every scheduling detail.

## Experimental logic

Replacing the inference engine with DFSan asks whether the dependency source helps within the surrounding system. Removing byte prioritization and conformance-guided evolution explains downstream uses. Coverage and crash growth over time reveal that early and late rankings can differ. The analysis acknowledges that complete ground-truth taint information is unavailable.

## Detail placement

Keep finite pilot mutations, the meaning of conformance, and retaining improved seeds on already visited paths in the method. Describe valid starting seeds and the distinction between per-run means and accumulated bug totals. Baseline reimplementations and the different worker configurations in the QSYM comparison matter to interpretation; routine interfaces can be shortened.

## Interpretation

Observed dependencies do not imply complete dependency recovery; the pilot can miss effects. More detected dependencies are not an absolute accuracy measurement without ground truth. The 105 findings include 25 already known privately to vendors, with 41 CVEs among the remaining 80. A late coverage advantage does not prove the baseline became trapped in a mathematical local optimum.
