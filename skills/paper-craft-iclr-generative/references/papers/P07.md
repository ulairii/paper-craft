# P07: Flow Matching for Generative Modeling

Yaron Lipman; Ricky T. Q. Chen; Heli Ben-Hamu; Maximilian Nickel; Matthew Le. **ICLR 2023**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PqvMRDCJT9t) · [Full text read: arXiv:2210.02747v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02747v2).

Source locations: Sections 3–4 and 6; Theorems 1–3; Tables 1–2; Figures 5–7; Appendix E.

## Writing move: Move from an ideal objective to a computable one

Flow Matching first defines the vector field it would like to fit, explains why that target is inaccessible, then constructs conditional targets with equivalent expected gradients. The theory answers the reader’s next question at each stage. A second distinction separates the training objective from the choice of probability path, making the two contributions easier to appreciate.

## Experimental logic

The matched-architecture comparison includes score matching, Flow Matching on a diffusion path, and Flow Matching on an OT conditional path. This separates objective and path choices. Training curves show convergence behavior; sampling-error and FID curves ask whether the resulting field is easier to integrate. Super-resolution adds a conditional application with its own perceptual-versus-reconstruction tradeoff.

## Detail placement

Keep conditional versus marginal fields, the computable regression target, and the sampling procedure in the main method. Put long continuity-equation derivations and schedule tables in the appendix. When comparing training efficiency, identify batch size and model size rather than equating update count with compute.

## Interpretation

Conditional Gaussian OT paths do not imply globally optimal marginal transport. Simulation-free describes training, not sampling. Numerical error against a high-step trajectory differs from sample quality. The super-resolution table improves FID and IS while reducing PSNR and SSIM; describe that difference instead of claiming every metric improves.
