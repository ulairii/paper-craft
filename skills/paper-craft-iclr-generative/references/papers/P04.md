# P04: Large Scale GAN Training for High Fidelity Natural Image Synthesis

Andrew Brock; Jeff Donahue; Karen Simonyan. **ICLR 2019**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=B1xsqj09Fm) · [Full text read: arXiv:1809.11096v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.11096v2).

Source locations: Sections 1 and 3–5; Tables 1–3; Appendices C–I.

## Writing move: Make a tradeoff part of the contribution

BigGAN connects three contributions: scaling improves synthesis, truncation controls fidelity versus variety, and experiments characterize instability at that scale. The last contribution remains valuable even though the paper does not solve collapse. It also credits inherited components and calls its skip-latent change a modification, allowing the combination and its demonstrated behavior to carry the novelty.

## Experimental logic

Table 1 develops a sequence of design changes; Table 2 reports different truncation operating points. The singular-value observations are followed by interventions: suppressing generator spectral explosion does not prevent collapse, while strong discriminator regularization stabilizes training at a quality cost. This progression turns an attractive explanation into an investigated question instead of treating correlation as the final answer.

## Detail placement

Keep scale, conditioning, checkpoint selection, and the chosen truncation point visible with the results. Move full architectures and alternative latent distributions to supporting material. Appendix H is a good model for concise negative results: name the setting and acknowledge that the exploratory evaluations were less extensive.

## Interpretation

Do not combine the minimum FID and maximum IS from different operating points into one result. Fewer iterations at larger batches do not establish cheaper training. JFT uses a different evaluation classifier, so its scores are not directly comparable to ImageNet. Later collapse does not erase the synthesis result, but stronger quality does not imply stable training.
