## Writing move: Organize a system around visible failure modes

SDXL turns a collection of design changes into a readable account by attaching each to a concrete problem: small training images, unwanted cropping, output aspect ratios, and local detail. Micro-conditioning is particularly clear because the unwanted effect and the information needed to control it are both easy to describe. The final pipeline then shows how the parts work together.

## Experimental logic

The size-conditioning comparison distinguishes discarding small images, retaining them without conditioning, and retaining them with conditioning. Autoencoder reconstruction and human image preferences answer different questions. The appendix reports disagreement between COCO FID and human preferences, making the evaluation criterion itself part of the explanation rather than hiding the less favorable metric.

## Detail placement

Keep the visible failure, conditioning signal, and inference use together. Explain the optional refiner and its added cost. Put bucket inventories and embedding code in supporting material. The autoencoder batch/EMA study belongs with its particular reconstruction protocol, not as direct evidence about final text-to-image preference.

## Interpretation

The overall model comparison does not isolate every architectural change. Four-way preference shares are not pairwise win rates. Specify the version and criterion in the Midjourney study. EMA and larger batches have different evidence: at similar wall time the smaller-batch, longer-trained autoencoder wins. Improved imagery does not mean text, hands, or attribute binding are solved.
