## Writing move: Expose an unused degree of freedom

DDIM identifies the key freedom before describing the sampler: the denoising objective depends on noisy marginals rather than the entire joint forward process. Different forward constructions can therefore motivate different sampling procedures using the same trained network. Equation 12 labels its terms by their roles, making the update understandable as prediction, direction, and noise rather than an unexplained formula.

## Experimental logic

The same checkpoint is sampled with different trajectory lengths and stochasticity levels. This isolates a sampling contribution. Quality-versus-step tables establish the operating region, and Figure 4 supplies wall-clock context. Fixed initial latents illustrate consistency; reconstruction error evaluates approximate inversion separately. These experiments extend the main idea instead of repeating the FID comparison.

## Detail placement

Keep checkpoint reuse, sampling steps, noise setting, and the relevant equivalence assumptions in the main explanation. Put dataset-specific time grids and extended interpolation galleries in supporting material. State that the formal weighted-objective argument uses unshared parameters across time, whereas the practical network is shared.

## Interpretation

The advantage is strongest at short trajectories. The full-step DDPM and a longer-step church setting provide counterexamples to universal superiority. Deterministic sampling does not make finite-step inversion exact, and equal-dimensional latent reconstruction is not a compression result.
