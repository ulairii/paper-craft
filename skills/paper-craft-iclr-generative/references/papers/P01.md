# P01: Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes

Diederik P Kingma; Max Welling. **ICLR 2014**. [Official paper](https://iclr.cc/archive/2014/conference-proceedings/) · [Full text read: arXiv:1312.6114v11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.6114v11).

Source locations: Sections 1–3 and 5; Figures 2–3; Appendices A–F.

## Writing move: Introduce a mathematical device through the obstacle it removes

AEVB begins with an intractable posterior and the need to learn from large datasets. Reparameterization then has a clear job: turn sampling into a differentiable transformation so that an estimator can be optimized. The recognition network has a second job: amortize inference across examples. This ordering makes two contributions distinguishable without presenting them as unrelated tricks. Define the probabilistic roles before introducing the estimator and its implementation.

## Experimental logic

The wake-sleep comparison uses the same recognition architecture. Figure 2 follows the variational bound through training and across latent dimensions; Figure 3 asks a different question using marginal-likelihood estimates. The latter comparison is restricted to three latent dimensions because those estimates become unreliable in larger spaces. This is a useful example of designing the displayed evidence around what can be meaningfully measured.

## Detail placement

Keep the continuous-latent assumptions, estimator construction, and distinction between a bound and likelihood in the main text. Put the analytic KL derivation and the untested full-Bayesian extension in supporting material. Do not let an extension with no experiments displace the central contribution.

## Interpretation

Faster progress per exposure to examples does not by itself establish lower wall-clock cost. The full-Bayesian formulation in Appendix F is not an evaluated capability. State what the reported inference and learning experiments demonstrate rather than promising universally accurate posteriors.
