## Writing move: Give related stages different explanatory jobs

Rectified Flow motivates straight, constant-speed trajectories through their numerical consequence: coarse integration becomes accurate. It distinguishes learning the first flow, reflowing the induced coupling, and distilling a final mapping. This is effective method organization because each stage changes a different object. The geometric intuition precedes the formal analysis of marginal preservation and transport costs.

## Experimental logic

Toy trajectories illustrate the construction; image experiments measure straightness and compare quality across sampling budgets. Table 1 separately reports the first flow, additional reflows, and distilled variants. Reflow improves the low-step regime while worsening fully integrated results through accumulated estimation error. The mixed result explains why the method is useful instead of undermining a universal claim.

## Detail placement

Keep the three stages and the source of reflow pairs in the method. The one-step image configuration uses an LPIPS distillation loss, so it should not be described as the unmodified least-squares algorithm alone. Put repeated galleries and optimizer choices in the appendix; identify the learned feature weighting used for image translation.

## Interpretation

Non-increasing convex transport costs do not imply globally optimal transport. The straightening theorem bounds the best value over a sequence, not monotonic improvement of every practical network. One-step results require additional training and are not the same as the first learned flow. Transport geometry alone does not guarantee semantic correspondence.
