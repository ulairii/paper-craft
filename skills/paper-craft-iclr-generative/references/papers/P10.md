## Writing move: Distinguish optimizing a score from satisfying the underlying goal

DDPO opens with a mismatch between distribution matching and a user’s downstream objective. It then explains why treating denoising as a sequence gives tractable per-step likelihoods for policy gradients. The reward-function section progresses from simple measurable objectives to learned aesthetic and language–image rewards, making the generality concrete without claiming every downstream task was tested.

## Experimental logic

The experiment section asks three explicit questions: optimizer comparison, VLM-derived rewards, and generalization to unseen prompts. Reward-query curves fit the stated resource bottleneck. Appendix E varies interleaving to investigate an alternative explanation for the optimizer gap. Generalization evidence combines examples with reward curves for held-out prompt groups.

## Detail placement

Keep the sequential formulation, reward definition, and evaluated prompt distribution in the main text. Explain that VLM rewards reuse pretrained supervision rather than requiring new annotations. Put full prompt lists and optimizer tables in supporting material. Qualitative checkpoint selection and the use of different hardware matter when discussing sustained improvement or runtime.

## Interpretation

Higher learned reward is not automatically better human alignment. Appendix A shows reward exploitation and selects qualitative checkpoints before deterioration. The main comparison measures reward queries, not identical training compute. Per-step likelihoods are tractable; this does not make the marginal image likelihood exact. Generalization beyond the tested prompt families remains open.
