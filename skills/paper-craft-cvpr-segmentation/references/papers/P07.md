## Writing move: Use an analogy that changes the computation

PointRend treats segmentation as rendering. The analogy earns its place because smooth interiors and rapidly changing boundaries suggest different sampling densities. It leads directly to adaptive subdivision, a point representation, and a point-wise predictor. The title communicates a new computational view rather than just naming a module.

## Experimental logic

The main comparison combines output quality and the feasibility of higher resolution. Re-evaluation with finer annotations helps explain gains that coarse masks can obscure. The sampling study shows that concentrating exclusively on uncertain regions is worse than maintaining some coverage, giving the training rule an interpretable balance.

## Detail placement

Keep the distinction between iterative inference and non-iterative training sampling explicit. Explain why fine features alone cannot distinguish overlapping instances at the same point and why coarse predictions supply region context. A toy subdivision step is more useful here than a large undifferentiated layer diagram.

## Interpretation

The paper discusses overlap metrics’ limited sensitivity to boundaries alongside qualitative examples. That is a reason to explain the evidence’s resolution, not permission to dismiss an unfavorable metric. Parameter saturation also explains why extra point predictions eventually give little benefit.
