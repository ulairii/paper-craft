## Writing move: Show why combining successful systems is not enough

Mask DINO turns a natural question into a diagnosis: why does simply attaching a segmentation head to a detector, or a detection head to a segmentation model, work poorly? The explanation distinguishes region-level position regression from pixel-level representation. Query selection, denoising, and matching then become ways to align the two tasks.

## Experimental logic

The unsuccessful simple combinations appear before the detailed method. Later stage-wise mask and box comparisons explain mask-enhanced initialization. Training individual versus combined tasks addresses cooperation directly, while detection pretraining tests a separate benefit of the shared framework: access to another source of training data.

## Detail placement

Distinguish inherited DINO operations from the added mask path, as the overview does. Explain why initial masks can provide useful boxes before detailing initialization. Separate the local method improvement from the larger-data result so the reader knows what each experiment teaches.

## Interpretation

Better joint box-and-mask learning does not mean all segmentation tasks help one another in a single trained model; the conclusion names that limitation. The story is strongest when cooperation refers to a particular representation or training benefit rather than a blanket promise.
