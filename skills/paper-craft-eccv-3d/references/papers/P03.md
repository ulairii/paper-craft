## Writing move: Make the experimental framework itself a contribution

PointContrast asks why pretraining has not transferred to 3D as reliably as it has elsewhere. Its answer combines scene-level observations, point correspondences, and a shared backbone. Presenting the transfer question before the contrastive loss makes the contribution larger and clearer than a claim of another objective.

## Experimental logic

Scratch comparisons on the same backbone separate representation transfer from architecture changes. Label-fraction experiments explain when pretraining matters most. Longer target-task training and two augmentations of a full scene probe alternative explanations for the multi-view benefit. They do not equalize every aspect of total pretraining compute.

## Detail placement

Keep how correspondences arise from aligned views and what unlabeled means: semantic labels are absent, but geometric alignment is available. Explain which pretrained components each downstream task retains. Put detailed augmentation ranges in supporting material unless augmentation is the proposed insight.

## Interpretation

A narrow gap to supervised pretraining in one setting does not close that gap throughout 3D learning. The advantage of naturally different views supports their usefulness; attributing it solely to noise or diversity would require a more isolating comparison.
