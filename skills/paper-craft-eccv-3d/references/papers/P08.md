Yingfei Liu and Tiancai Wang and Xiangyu Zhang and Jian Sun. **ECCV 2022**. [Official paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/4420_ECCV_2022_paper.php) · [Full text](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136870523.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 3.2–3.5 and 4.3–4.5; Tables 1–5; Figure 5.

## Writing move: Sell a simpler interface by locating where geometry enters

PETR moves geometric information into image-feature position embeddings. Rather than repeatedly explaining projection inside query sampling, it constructs 3D coordinates along camera rays and encodes them with the image features. The contribution becomes a clear change in the interface between geometry and detection.

## Experimental logic

The large difference between two-dimensional and three-dimensional position information addresses the central idea. Smaller differences among addition, concatenation, and auxiliary encodings help identify which choices deserve emphasis. Training curves also disclose slower early convergence rather than hiding it behind final accuracy.

## Detail placement

Explain the sampled frustum coordinates, calibration, normalization, and channel-wise encoding. The representation remains on the image grid; it is neither a dense BEV volume nor an estimate of the true depth at each pixel. Give model and pretraining variants beside headline results.

## Interpretation

Removing query-dependent projection does not remove the need for camera geometry. A failed convolutional encoding in the tested setup does not establish that all spatial convolution is unsuitable. Speed comparisons across different GPUs cannot explain a controlled efficiency advantage.

## Original writing illustration

Before: We eliminate geometry processing through an entirely calibration-free detector.

After: We encode calibrated 3D coordinates in image features, allowing detection queries to use geometry without repeating query-dependent projection at each attention step.
