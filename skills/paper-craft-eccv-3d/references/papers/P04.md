## Writing move: Use redundant geometry to motivate complementary evidence

H3DNet explains why a center alone may be a weak geometric summary of a partially observed object. Centers, face centers, and edge centers provide different constraints. The method follows that argument through primitive prediction, geometric proposal fitting, and learned refinement, instead of introducing three heads without a shared reason.

## Experimental logic

The wider VoteNet control addresses whether additional capacity explains the gain. Stronger localization thresholds and thin-object examples connect the result to box geometry. Supplementary primitive observability and error-correlation measurements make complementarity more concrete, while revealing different behavior across datasets.

## Detail placement

Keep the upright-box assumption, observable-primitive supervision, and robust fitting objective in the main explanation. Local-minimum differentiation needs a suitable local branch; a full derivative belongs in the supplement. Report the extra refinement cost when discussing practicality.

## Interpretation

Empirical error complementarity motivates combining constraints; it is not a guarantee that every primitive improves every scene. The covariance discussion is more useful as geometric evidence than as a sweeping statistical claim. A stronger detector can be persuasive without being cost-free.
