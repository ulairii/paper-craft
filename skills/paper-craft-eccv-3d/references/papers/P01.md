## Writing move: Derive the operator from the sampling problem

SpiderCNN starts with convolution and asks how to evaluate a learned filter on irregular samples. The radial step function and polynomial then have recognizable roles: coarse radial structure and local variation. This progression makes the filter a response to the input geometry rather than an arbitrary architectural ingredient.

## Experimental logic

Filter alternatives and neighborhood choices test the operator, while classification and part segmentation show its use. The FPFH fitting experiment offers a separate view of local approximation. Distinguish input conditions: normals improve classification, and the sparsity study retrains at each point count rather than dropping points from a fixed trained model.

## Detail placement

Keep relative coordinates, the filter construction, and the k-nearest-neighbor approximation to radial neighborhoods in the method. Routine channel counts can follow later. Normals and known object categories belong beside the experiments that use them.

## Interpretation

A successful polynomial filter does not show that every MLP filter is unsuitable. Rotation augmentation supports robustness under the tested distribution, not exact rotation invariance. State best-run and average accuracy as different summaries.
