## Writing move: Preserve useful flexibility while changing the costly operation

Consistency Models makes its design goal more interesting than simple acceleration: support one-step generation while retaining a route to better quality and editing through additional steps. The definition maps points on the same probability-flow trajectory to a common endpoint. This property explains both the name and the training signal.

## Experimental logic

The paper separates distillation from training directly on data. For distillation, a common teacher and comparisons using both perceptual and pixel losses clarify which gains belong to the method. The direct-training experiments establish a different capability. Few-step curves show the flexibility promised in the introduction rather than relying solely on the best one-step number.

## Detail placement

Keep the endpoint boundary condition and its role in excluding a trivial constant solution. Distinguish training discretization from sampling evaluations. Explain the teacher dependence of distillation; do not attach its headline score to the independently trained model. Detailed schedules belong in the appendix, where continuous-time experiments also have different initialization conditions.

## Interpretation

One-step performance is strong in the stated comparison class, not better than every GAN or iterative diffusion model. Editing demonstrations use a distilled model and can involve multiple evaluations. Theoretical accuracy statements require their regularity and loss conditions; practical training does not automatically satisfy them. This separation lets the paper sell both speed and flexibility accurately.
