# P02: Variational Inference with Normalizing Flows

Rezende, Danilo; Mohamed, Shakir. **ICML 2015**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/rezende15.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/rezende15.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 2–4; Algorithm 1; Section 6; Figures 3–4; Tables 2–3; supplementary Section A.

## Writing move: Narrow the problem to the approximation that limits the model

Normalizing Flows identifies a specific remaining obstacle after scalable variational training: a simple posterior family may be too restrictive. The method transforms a tractable base density through a sequence of invertible maps. Expressiveness and inexpensive density calculation are developed together, making the computational design part of the scientific idea.

## Experimental logic

Two-dimensional target densities show what the transformations can represent. The image experiments then separately examine the variational bound, posterior discrepancy, and estimated likelihood as flow length increases. This separates a better approximation from a better fitted generative model more clearly than a single final score would.

## Detail placement

Explain the change-of-variables correction and why the chosen maps have inexpensive Jacobians. The flow enriches the approximate posterior in this paper; it should not be described as though the entire generative architecture has been replaced. Put the detailed invertibility constraints in supporting material while retaining their purpose in the method.

## Interpretation

The asymptotic richness of a family is not a guarantee that a finite trained flow recovers the true posterior. The matched NICE comparison and the differently configured HVI comparison serve different roles, which the paper acknowledges. The CIFAR experiment uses image patches. Report the computational cost of the flow separately from the inference network's cost.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Arbitrarily expressive flows guarantee exact Bayesian inference at linear cost.

After: A sequence of tractable invertible transformations enriches the approximate posterior. Increasing the flow length improves the evaluated inference quality while adding a controlled computational cost.
