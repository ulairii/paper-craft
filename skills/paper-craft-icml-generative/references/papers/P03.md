## Writing move: Make a mathematical choice matter through a simple counterexample

WGAN explains why the choice of distance matters before presenting the algorithm. The parallel-lines example turns distributional topology into a concrete optimization problem. The dual formulation then motivates a critic, and the experiments return to two practical promises: more informative learning curves and less fragile training.

## Experimental logic

The Gaussian-mixture experiment makes mode behavior observable. Image experiments vary generator architecture, testing whether training still works when useful architectural biases are weakened. Loss curves are paired with samples so the reader can assess what the numerical signal means. These tests serve the stability story rather than merely displaying the best image grid.

## Detail placement

Keep the critic constraint, approximate optimization, and meaning of the loss in the main text. Detailed topology and proofs can remain in the supplement. Explain that the finite critic estimates a restricted objective whose scale depends on its architecture and constraints.

## Interpretation

The paper explicitly declines to call its critic loss a universal quantitative evaluation metric. Its own high-learning-rate and momentum failures also delimit the stability claim. The absence of observed mode collapse in these experiments is useful evidence, but does not prove that every finite WGAN training run avoids collapse. Borrow the example-to-principle argument, not the broadest wording in the abstract.
