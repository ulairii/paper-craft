## Writing move: Use an unexpected tradeoff to discover the useful modification

Improved DDPM begins with precise unanswered questions about likelihood, coverage, and sampling cost. It connects each change to a diagnosis: early steps matter disproportionately for likelihood, a schedule can waste steps after destroying useful information, and direct likelihood optimization can be noisy. Small modifications become a coherent contribution through these explanations.

## Experimental logic

The surprising failure of the direct variational objective leads to gradient-noise measurements and then importance sampling. The result does not erase the tradeoff: the best likelihood objective has worse FID, motivating the hybrid objective. Sampling curves examine both intermediate and mature checkpoints, while precision and recall probe coverage beyond a single quality score.

## Detail placement

Explain the variance parameterization and the jobs of the hybrid objective's terms. State the evaluation's conditioning and sample count where needed, because unconditional and class-conditional FIDs use different protocols. Leave long architecture specifications in supporting material. The supplementary experiment that combines models at different timesteps belongs with the explanation of the likelihood–quality tradeoff.

## Interpretation

Learned variance enables fewer sampling steps without fine-tuning, but DDIM is better in the very-low-step regime. Recall gains do not imply higher precision. Reported likelihoods use a variational bound, and the scaling curves are preliminary observations rather than universal laws. The paper is strongest when describing a useful operating tradeoff.
