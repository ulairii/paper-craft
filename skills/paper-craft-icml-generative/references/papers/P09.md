## Writing move: Locate the failure before proposing a new model

Reduce, Reuse, Recycle asks why composing useful diffusion models can produce poor samples. It identifies a mismatch: the product of diffused distributions is generally not the diffusion of their product. This diagnosis motivates a different sampling procedure. The energy parameterization follows because it enables further composition operators and corrected samplers.

## Experimental logic

Known two-dimensional distributions make the failure and repair inspectable. An equal-evaluation reverse-diffusion baseline tests whether extra computation alone explains the gain. Model-parameterization and sampler comparisons then separate two proposed changes. More complex spatial constraints and image tasks extend the argument, while text and tapestry examples illustrate new uses.

## Detail placement

Explain which distribution each sampler targets and what information the energy parameterization adds. Mixture weights depend on relative normalizers, and finite MCMC is still an approximation. The additional backward computation and sampling cost belong in the tradeoff discussion. Keep the full sampler tuning and compositional prompt lists in the appendix.

## Interpretation

The energy parameterization is not universally better: its ordinary unconditional generation can be worse, and the best classifier accuracy and best FID need not come from the same configuration. Composing existing compatible models can avoid retraining, whereas changing their parameterization is a separate training choice. Disjoint-support examples also reveal when the desired product is problematic. Sell the newly enabled control at its stated cost.
