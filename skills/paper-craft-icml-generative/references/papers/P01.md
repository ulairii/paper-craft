# P01: Stochastic Backpropagation and Approximate Inference in Deep Generative Models

Rezende, Danilo Jimenez; Mohamed, Shakir; Wierstra, Daan. **ICML 2014**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/rezende14.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/rezende14.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 2–5; Figure 1–2; Algorithm 1; Table 1; supplementary Sections A–F and H.

## Writing move: Derive a practical learning procedure from the inference bottleneck

Stochastic Backpropagation gives the reader three connected goals: hierarchical models, tractable inference, and easy sampling. It explains the generative process before introducing the recognition model that makes learning practical. The algorithm follows from the objective and gradient identities, so its components have a reason to exist.

## Experimental logic

The posterior diagnostic precedes the application results. In a small latent space, the paper compares the recognition distribution with an importance-sampling approximation and shows what a structured covariance captures beyond a diagonal one. Likelihood, generated examples, visualization, and imputation then demonstrate different uses of the same probabilistic model.

## Detail placement

Keep the bottom-up recognition pass, top-down generation pass, and meaning of the variational objective in the main explanation. The low-rank covariance construction matters because it changes the expressiveness–cost tradeoff. Detailed identities and training recipes can follow in the appendix.

## Interpretation

The paper offers competitive likelihoods rather than a win over every model in Table 1. A likelihood estimate, a variational bound, and visual sample quality are distinct evidence. Imputation uses an iterative procedure, even though the recognition network gives fast inference; the approximate posterior does not make every conditional sample exact. Credit established Gaussian identities and the acknowledged concurrent work instead of claiming all parts are unprecedented.
