Zheng, Hongkai; Nie, Weili; Vahdat, Arash; Azizzadenesheli, Kamyar; Anandkumar, Anima. **ICML 2023**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zheng23d.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zheng23d/zheng23d.pdf).

## Writing move: Explain what parallelism removes and what it still costs

DSNO reformulates fast sampling as learning an operator from initial noise to an entire time-indexed trajectory. Temporal Fourier blocks make that proposal concrete. The observed concentration of temporal spectral energy motivates representing the trajectory with relatively few time locations. The paper connects a numerical bottleneck to an architectural inductive bias.

## Experimental logic

The temporal-block ablation tests the proposed coupling. Resolution, weighting, and loss comparisons explain the final operating point. Trajectory and endpoint comparisons show what the learned operator preserves. Runtime measurements supplement evaluation counts, since one larger parallel call costs more than one call to the original backbone.

## Detail placement

State that parallelism is across diffusion time, not arbitrary output pixels or independent video frames. Describe the teacher-generated trajectory dataset and its upfront cost. Temporal resolution is distinct from spatial image resolution. Put detailed operator background in the appendix after the main text has explained the input and output of the learned map.

## Interpretation

One evaluation is not a universal unit of computation: the measured call is slower than the original backbone's single call. The learned sampler improves the stated one-step baselines but does not retain all iterative-teacher quality. The unseen-time-resolution demonstration is qualitative and imperfect. Guided sampling is left for future work, and contemporary comparisons should include relevant concurrent methods such as Consistency Models rather than repeat a timeless state-of-the-art claim.
