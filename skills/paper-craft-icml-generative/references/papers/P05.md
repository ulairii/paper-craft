## Writing move: Let conflicting evaluations sharpen the contribution

GLIDE combines a user-facing goal with a focused scientific comparison: generate realistic images that follow free-form text, then determine which guidance strategy better serves that goal. Editing is introduced as a way to refine complex scenes that one-shot prompting may not reliably produce. The paper credits the existing guidance ideas and studies their use in this setting.

## Experimental logic

Guidance sweeps expose fidelity–diversity tradeoffs. CLIP score favors CLIP guidance, while human judgments favor classifier-free guidance for both realism and caption agreement. The paper treats that conflict as informative. DALL-E comparisons additionally vary reranking and pass GLIDE outputs through DALL-E's image codec, testing whether a preference survives those alternative explanations.

## Detail placement

Keep realism and prompt agreement as separate outcomes. The scale chosen for FID differs from the scale chosen for human preference. Describe the base model and upsampler together when stating system size. Inpainting uses fine-tuning, and the supplementary inpainting comparison uses reranking even though the main generation comparison does not.

## Interpretation

The disagreement with human judgments supports concern about the automatic metric; it does not by itself prove a particular adversarial mechanism. The appendix also shows a context–prompt tradeoff in inpainting. Selected unusual-prompt failures delimit composition capability. The released smaller filtered model is distinct from the system producing the headline results.
