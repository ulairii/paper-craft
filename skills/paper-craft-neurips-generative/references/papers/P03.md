## Writing move: Diagnose a failed implementation of a good principle before proposing its replacement

Improved Training of Wasserstein GANs preserves the motivation for Wasserstein learning and identifies weight clipping as the practical obstacle. Toy examples make restricted critic capacity and unstable gradients understandable before the gradient penalty is introduced. The story is a targeted repair of an existing principle, with a simple objective that follows naturally from the diagnosis.

## Experimental logic

Fixed-generator examples isolate critic behavior. Randomly sampled architectures test robustness beyond one successful configuration. Image and character experiments then establish practical reach. A matched-optimizer comparison isolates the penalty more cleanly than the headline configurations, while larger successful models show what improved training makes possible. Wall-clock curves give stability an operational meaning.

## Detail placement

Keep interpolation sampling and the gradient target in the objective explanation. Explain why critic batch normalization conflicts with a per-example penalty and why layer normalization is a compatible alternative. Separate the ideal optimal-coupling property from the sampled soft penalty used in training. Longer sample galleries and complete architecture lists can remain supplementary.

## Interpretation

The penalty encourages the intended gradient behavior on sampled points; it is not a global Lipschitz guarantee. Stability across many tested architectures is stronger evidence than one example, but not universality. Training may be slower than a successful DCGAN configuration even when it improves substantially over clipping. Character experiments establish a limited continuous-output demonstration.
