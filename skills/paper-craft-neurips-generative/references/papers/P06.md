## Writing move: Turn disagreement between evaluation criteria into an explanatory result

DDPM connects diffusion training to denoising score matching, then shows how parameterization and loss weighting make that connection practically useful. The simple training and sampling algorithms give a complex probabilistic method an accessible center. The paper does more than report good images: it uses the contrast between sample quality and likelihood to develop a rate-distortion interpretation.

## Experimental logic

Table 2 compares parameterizations and objectives together, revealing an interaction rather than a single universally better choice. Sample-quality results establish the capability. Likelihood and progressive compression analysis ask what information the model spends bits representing. This sequence turns a metric mismatch into a scientific explanation rather than hiding the less favorable metric.

## Detail placement

Keep the noise-prediction target, loss weighting, variance choice, and iterative sampling cost in the main account. State which reference set defines FID. Put complete layer settings and additional sample grids in supporting material. Explain that the compression construction relies on a theoretically available but impractical coding procedure.

## Interpretation

The strongest sample results do not mean the model leads on likelihood or every dataset. Noise prediction alone under the original variational weighting is not the full explanation for the improvement. A progressive lossy coding interpretation is not an implemented practical compressor. The contribution is a useful connection among training, synthesis, and information allocation.
