## Writing move: Sell a mathematical building block through the limitation it removes

Neural Spline Flows begins with the restricted expressiveness of common scalar transformations. Rational-quadratic splines provide flexible monotonic maps with an analytic inverse. The paper credits earlier spline constructions and explains why this particular choice fits normalizing flows. The contribution is a reusable transformation, so the narrative follows its insertion into several model families.

## Experimental logic

Toy densities show what a small number of flexible transformations can represent. Tabular density estimation tests practical likelihood gains. VAE experiments ask whether more expressive priors and posteriors matter, while image experiments test parameter efficiency. A strengthened affine baseline makes the comparison informative. Similar spline and affine VAE outcomes reveal a setting where additional flexibility has limited value.

## Detail placement

Keep monotonicity, boundary behavior, and analytic inversion in the core explanation. Distinguish coupling from autoregressive implementations because their forward and inverse costs differ. Place the full algebra and bin-search details after readers understand the transformation. Match the size of the claim to the capacity comparison being made.

## Interpretation

The image result is competitive likelihood with fewer parameters, not uniformly better likelihood. Training updates can be slower despite parameter savings. A proposed relationship between dataset size, dimension, and useful flexibility is an interpretation, not an isolated causal finding. Reusing a known numerical construction can still be a substantial modeling contribution.
