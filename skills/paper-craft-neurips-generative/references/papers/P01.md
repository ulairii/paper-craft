## Writing move: Introduce a new training principle through the roles of its participants

GANs makes an unfamiliar learning procedure understandable through two familiar roles: producing examples and distinguishing generated examples from data. The brief analogy leads quickly to the actual optimization problem. A theory section explains the ideal equilibrium, while the experiments establish that the principle can train useful generative models. This is a strong foundational story without requiring a universal best-score claim.

## Experimental logic

Samples, nearest neighbors, and a quantitative proxy provide complementary evidence of viability. Table 2 is unusually useful rhetorically: it compares operational properties of modeling approaches, making the reason to explore adversarial learning explicit. The density comparison uses a Parzen-window estimate rather than exact likelihood; the paper discusses why that proxy is problematic in high dimensions.

## Detail placement

Keep the generator and discriminator objectives, alternating updates, and practical non-saturating generator loss in the main explanation. The practical loss differs from the minimax expression, so it deserves a sentence. Distinguish the ideal nonparametric discriminator argument from finite-network optimization. Potential conditional generation and inference extensions belong after the demonstrated contribution.

## Interpretation

The evidence supports a viable learning principle and competitive samples. It does not prove convergence of arbitrary neural training or absence of memorization. The nearest-neighbor examples illustrate novelty locally. Real-valued MNIST likelihood estimates should not be described as directly comparable to binary-data likelihoods.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our adversarial paradigm guarantees optimal generation and surpasses all density models.

After: Adversarial training learns a generator through feedback from a discriminator; the experiments demonstrate competitive synthesis without requiring an explicit tractable generator density.
