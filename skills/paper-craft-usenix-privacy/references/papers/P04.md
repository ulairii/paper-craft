# P04: Rendered Private: Making GLSL Execution Uniform to Prevent WebGL-based Browser Fingerprinting

Shujiang Wu; Song Li; Yinzhi Cao; Ningfei Wang. **USENIX Security 2019**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/wu) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec19fall_wu_prepub.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–7; floating-point examples, transformation stages and evaluation; Appendix A.

## Writing move: Make the required notion of equivalence explicit

Rendered Private distinguishes visually similar output from computationally identical output. A small floating-point example makes that difference tangible before UniGL’s transformation pipeline is introduced. The reader can then understand why ordinary graphics compatibility does not suffice for fingerprint resistance. Each stage addresses a source of rendering variation.

## Experimental logic

The study combines targeted rendering examples, collected fingerprints, application checks, and performance measurements. Seventeen known rendering tests producing uniform outputs across 656 collected fingerprints supports a concrete compatibility claim. Stage costs and successive optimizations explain how the implementation becomes practical, while application examples address the reason users need WebGL in the first place.

## Detail placement

Keep the meaning of uniformity, the CPU/GPU division, and relevant execution assumptions in the method. Explain that the evaluated transformation is integrated into the demonstration setup. Full shader details fit later. Frame-rate limits and the distinction between CPU package power and total system energy are necessary when those measurements support efficiency claims.

## Interpretation

Uniformity on the evaluated programs is not a proof for every GLSL program or every fingerprinting channel. Similar frame rates near a display limit do not imply zero overhead. Visually acceptable results can differ numerically from the original renderer. Browser-native protection and a fully deployed adversarial extension require evidence beyond the demonstration.
