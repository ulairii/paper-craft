# P02: Fp-Scanner: The Privacy Implications of Browser Fingerprint Inconsistencies

Antoine Vastel; Pierre Laperdrix; Walter Rudametkin; Romain Rouvoy. **USENIX Security 2018**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/vastel) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-vastel.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–6; fingerprint consistency tests, Tables 6 and 9, evaluation and discussion.

## Writing move: Turn a defense’s internal inconsistency into the central observation

Fp-Scanner explains a subtle idea through relationships among attributes: changing one browser property can leave it inconsistent with others. Organizing the method around operating-system, browser, device, and rendering consistency gives the reader a useful vocabulary. The contribution is the inference enabled by these relationships, rather than the number of rules implemented.

## Experimental logic

The constructed collection covers seven working defenses and genuine fingerprints. Detection and attribute recovery answer separate questions. Examples of partial recovery explain what an inconsistency reveals, while difficult cases show why detecting a defense need not restore an exact browser version. The timing analysis separates cheap checks from rendering-related work.

## Detail placement

Keep the expected relationship and the observed contradiction beside representative tests. State the constructed collection and threshold selection when interpreting accuracy. Long browser-version combinations and complete rules can follow the main example. Report the actual timing boundary rather than treating server-side checks as the entire collection process.

## Interpretation

Perfect detection in the collected examples does not establish perfect real-world detection. Detectability alone is not longitudinal tracking: privacy depends on the remaining information, stability, and anonymity set. Attribute-family recovery and precise-version recovery should have different verbs. The source’s speculative consequences need not become measured outcomes in a new manuscript.
