# P09: When Sally Met Trackers: Web Tracking From the Users' Perspective

Savino Dambra; Iskander Sanchez-Rola; Leyla Bilge; Davide Balzarotti. **USENIX Security 2022**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/dambra) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-dambra.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–8; telemetry methodology, encounter curves, Tables 3 and 6 and coverage analyses; Appendices A–E.

## Writing move: Change the unit of measurement to answer a different question

When Sally Met Trackers asks what users encounter and when, instead of counting only websites containing trackers. The paper develops two linked stories: encounter frequency and potential knowledge of browsing activity. This change of unit makes familiar tracking infrastructure yield new observations. The contrast between website coverage and visit-weighted history is the central explanatory device.

## Experimental logic

Telemetry is joined with a separate crawl to estimate exposure. Per-user encounter curves reveal how quickly the observed weekly tracker set accumulates. Collaboration scenarios use unions of coverage to show asymmetric gains. Geographic follow-up examines sensitivity to crawl location. Sensitive-category analysis asks where exposure occurs without needing to establish a person’s actual medical condition or beliefs.

## Detail placement

Keep the opt-in Windows population, eight-day window, hourly granularity, and later crawl date visible. Distinguish unique domains, visit entries, people, and tracking organizations whenever the unit changes. Full geographic distributions and categorization details belong in the appendix. Complete-sharing scenarios need their assumption beside the result.

## Interpretation

Tracker presence in a later crawl estimates potential exposure rather than directly observing every historical transmission. A weekly encounter fraction is not a fraction of all trackers a user could ever encounter. Website category and vendor risk scores support associations, not causal effects of security choices or laws. Cookie recurrence modeling does not measure complete unlinkability after clearing cookies.
