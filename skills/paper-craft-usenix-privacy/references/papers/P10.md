# P10: WebGraph: Capturing Advertising and Tracking Information Flows for Robust Blocking

Sandra Siby; Umar Iqbal; Steven Englehardt; Zubair Shafiq; Carmela Troncoso. **USENIX Security 2022**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/siby) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-siby.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–7; cookie-sharing trace, graph comparison, Tables 1–3 and mutation experiments; Appendices A–F.

## Writing move: Sell robustness through the behavior a representation preserves

WebGraph first shows why mutable URL properties undermine a graph-based classifier, then introduces storage and sharing behavior as a more durable signal. A concrete cookie-sharing trace maps directly into added graph edges. This example gives the representation an operational meaning before the feature list appears. The contribution is a better basis for a decision, rather than graph complexity itself.

## Experimental logic

Representation-only and feature comparisons separate the benefit of the enriched graph from explicit flow features. Content mutations test the motivating weakness; more capable graph mutations test an adapted adversary. Evasion and unintended blocking are reported together. This experimental progression explains what improves and why the strongest attack changes the interpretation.

## Detail placement

Keep the offline adaptation, filter-list labels, content-free variant, attacker control, and graph-growth budget in the main text. Explain approximate flow capture with a trace. Full features and mutation pseudocode fit in the appendix. Runtime belongs to offline list curation; it is not a measured online browser delay.

## Interpretation

The content-free variant has lower clean accuracy than the content-using baseline, while reducing success of the colluding content attack from 96.62% to 8.34%. That is a useful tradeoff. Stronger graph attacks achieve much higher evasion, so 8.34% is not a universal robustness figure. Shared-value matching remains affected by some URL changes; full information-flow tracking is a proposal.
