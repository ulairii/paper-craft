# P03: Who Left Open the Cookie Jar? A Comprehensive Evaluation of Third-Party Cookie Policies

Gertjan Franken; Tom Van Goethem; Wouter Joosen. **USENIX Security 2018**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/franken) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-franken.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–6; cookie-policy test design, bypass categories and deployment observations; complete appendix.

## Writing move: Build an empirical taxonomy around causes, not a list of failures

The Cookie Jar study begins with a shared mechanism—requests carrying cookies—and explains why privacy policies and CSRF protections need different interpretations. Systematic tests become a taxonomy of policy, implementation, and browser-interface causes. This structure turns a large compatibility matrix into an argument about where policy enforcement breaks down.

## Experimental logic

The experiments vary request mechanisms and contexts across browsers and extensions. Follow-up crawling asks whether related mechanisms occur on real sites. The distinction matters: seeing an API in use does not show intentional use of a newly described bypass. Investigation of candidate DNS cases illustrates how a measurement can narrow its interpretation without losing its contribution.

## Detail placement

Keep expected policy behavior, request context, and whether cookies or entire requests are blocked in the main text. Explain representative causes and a remedy at the responsible layer. Full versions, all request compositions, and disclosure records fit in the appendix. A category-level positive result need not imply that every member behaves identically.

## Interpretation

A designed policy exception and an implementation defect call for different language. The study did not observe intentional use of the newly identified bypasses in its crawl; that is a bounded observation. Historical fixes and vendor responses describe their recorded status. Cookie delivery alone does not establish every downstream attack capability.

## Original writing illustration

Before: All privacy extensions are broken and websites widely exploit these vulnerabilities.

After: Systematic request-context tests reveal several distinct causes of cookie-policy bypass, while the deployment study examines where related browser mechanisms occur.
