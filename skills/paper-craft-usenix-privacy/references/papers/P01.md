# P01: A Privacy Analysis of Cross-device Tracking

Sebastian Zimmeck; Jie S. Li; Hyungtae Kim; Steven M. Bellovin; Tony Jebara. **USENIX Security 2017**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity17/technical-sessions/presentation/zimmeck) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-zimmeck.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–7; cross-device case study, matching experiment and inference analysis; Appendices A–B.

## Writing move: Separate an observable mechanism from its possible reach

The paper makes cross-device tracking concrete with a paired-device case before examining matching capability and ecosystem reach. Those are different questions, and the organization lets each supply a different part of the privacy argument. The feature design becomes meaningful because mobile and desktop activity expose different information. The story is about linking fragments of a person’s activity, rather than simply introducing another classifier.

## Experimental logic

The paired case includes a separate-IP control. A participant dataset then tests device matching, while finance and gender inference explore what combining devices adds. The unequal downstream results are instructive: extra data helps when the devices supply complementary information. A later crawl and tracker catalog estimate potential reach rather than directly observing every company linking every visit.

## Detail placement

Keep recruitment, device pairing, training/test separation, observed data fields, and the meaning of each measurement in the main text. The sample is largely university participants. Put the full field inventory and tracker list in the appendix. State that the reach crawl occurs later and that partnership estimates assume information sharing.

## Interpretation

The matching F1 of 0.91 belongs to a small specified test set, not the Internet population. Tracker presence, demonstrated matching capability, and observed cross-device ads support different statements. A failed reverse-direction observation does not show that the mechanism never operates. The historical transparency findings describe the study period.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our analysis proves that companies identify and profile every user across devices.

After: The paired-device case demonstrates a tracking path, while the matching and inference experiments show how combining device activity can increase the information available in the studied setting.
