# P06: Off-Policy Deep Reinforcement Learning without Exploration

Fujimoto, Scott; Meger, David; Precup, Doina. **ICML 2019**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/fujimoto19a.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/fujimoto19a/fujimoto19a.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 3–5; Figures 1–3; supplement A, D and F–G.

## Writing move: Make the data-collection constraint define the problem

BCQ makes offline learning concrete by removing the learner's ability to collect corrective experience. Its concurrent-buffer experiment is especially memorable: two agents can see the same data yet learn differently because their policies differ. The action constraint follows directly from this mismatch. Introduce such a setting change before describing a new model, so the reader understands why an existing algorithm cannot simply be reused.

## Experimental logic

Final-buffer, concurrent, expert, and noisy-demonstration settings vary the relation between behavior and learner. Return and value-estimation plots answer complementary questions. Behavioral cloning is a meaningful comparison on expert data; noisy demonstrations reveal the value of selecting useful behavior rather than copying everything.

## Detail placement

Keep the source of the batch and the absence of new training interactions prominent. Explain candidate generation, bounded perturbation, and value-based selection as a sequence. Move VAE background and layer dimensions to supporting material; retain the perturbation bound because it defines how far the policy can leave the data.

## Interpretation

The finite deterministic-MDP result requires a coherent batch; a learned continuous generator only approximates that constraint. Supplement D shows both successful DDPG learning on simple covered tasks and stable ensemble values without good imitation. These cases clarify why coverage and policy quality matter beyond a stable critic.
