Zheng, Qinqing; Zhang, Amy; Grover, Aditya. **ICML 2022**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/zheng22c.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/zheng22c/zheng22c.pdf).

## Writing move: Distinguish adaptation gains from final capability

ODT frames its advance as making a pretrained sequence policy useful for online adaptation. The missing capability is exploration, not sequence prediction alone. Stochastic actions, sequence-level entropy, trajectory replay, and achieved-return relabeling are introduced as changes needed by that transition. The abstract emphasizes finetuning gains while describing absolute performance as competitive.

## Experimental logic

Offline and online columns expose starting quality alongside final return. Stochastic-policy and relabeling comparisons address the proposed adaptation mechanism; fixed versus curriculum return targets explain a practical design choice. Additional context-length and positional-embedding experiments show that sequence modeling choices interact with the task.

## Detail placement

Keep offline data access, the online interaction budget, and exploration versus evaluation return targets visible. Initializations matter: appendix C uses different pretraining schedules for standalone offline models and finetuning, so do not assume every offline score is the exact starting checkpoint. Full hyperparameter tables and unsuccessful heuristics can remain in the appendix.

## Interpretation

A larger reported improvement does not by itself prove a better adaptation rule under matched starting policies. Some tasks show little or negative gain. Section 4.3 explicitly assumes convergence for its analysis rather than proving the complete algorithm converges. Describe the observed training dynamics accordingly.
