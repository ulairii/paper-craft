Schulman, John; Levine, Sergey; Abbeel, Pieter; Jordan, Michael; Moritz, Philipp. **ICML 2015**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schulman15.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/schulman15.pdf).

## Writing move: Make the approximation ladder part of the contribution

TRPO makes a difficult optimization story readable by moving from a policy-improvement argument to a usable update in explicit stages. Section 6 recaps the substitutions: a penalty becomes a constraint, a maximum divergence becomes an average, and advantages are estimated from data. This lets the reader see both the theoretical motivation and the practical contribution. Borrow that ladder when your algorithm is inspired by a guarantee but does not implement its exact assumptions.

## Experimental logic

Section 8 states three questions before presenting results: sampling variants, the effect of the trust-region choice, and performance on larger problems. Each comparison has a purpose. Locomotion and Atari demonstrate different aspects of applicability; they are not interchangeable entries in a benchmark count.

## Detail placement

Keep the optimization objective, the meaning of the constraint, and the approximations in the main text. Put Fisher-vector calculations and full settings in supporting material unless they are your innovation. State the reset access required by vine sampling where the variant is introduced.

## Interpretation

Figure 4 averages five locomotion runs, whereas Table 1 explicitly reports single Atari runs. The Atari evidence supports versatility with competitive results on some games, not reliable superiority everywhere. Preserve the distinction between the ideal improvement theorem and the practical algorithm's observed behavior.
