## Writing move: Build an empirical contribution around an interaction

This replay study earns its narrative through an unexpected contrast: larger memory helps one learner but not another. Definitions of capacity, policy age, and replay ratio turn a vague tuning question into a tractable scientific question. The story progresses from a phenomenon to the component that enables it, then to competing explanations. A useful finding can be the interaction itself; a new named algorithm is not required.

## Experimental logic

The grid separates changes in capacity from changes in policy age. Additive DQN and ablative Rainbow experiments locate the role of multi-step returns in the response to larger buffers. Lower-discount and reduced-stochasticity experiments then test explanations, including one that receives only partial support.

## Detail placement

Explain which resource is held fixed: the grid fixes gradient updates and batch size while collected experience can differ. Keep the definition of policy age in gradient steps nearby. Detailed per-game curves can move to the supplement, but sparse-reward exceptions belong in the interpretation because they qualify the trend.

## Interpretation

The experiment measures improvement from increasing memory, not which component produces the best absolute agent. Policy age is a proxy for off-policyness, and the implementation cannot isolate every property of the data distribution. Reduced stochasticity weakens but does not eliminate gains, supporting a partial account rather than a complete explanation.
