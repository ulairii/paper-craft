# P03: A Distributional Perspective on Reinforcement Learning

Marc G. Bellemare; Will Dabney; R{\'e. **ICML 2017**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/bellemare17a.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/bellemare17a/bellemare17a.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1, 3–6; Figures 3–7; supplement A–B and Figures 14–18.

## Writing move: Change the learning target, then explain why that matters

The distributional paper introduces its central object by contrasting a random return with its expectation. This short conceptual shift carries the paper before the notation expands. It credits earlier distributional work and locates its contribution in approximate reinforcement learning. Use this pattern when your advance changes what a learner predicts: define the new object, identify information the old target discards, then explain the learning consequence.

## Experimental logic

An atom-count study links representational richness to performance. Atari comparisons establish utility, while action-conditioned return distributions illustrate information that a scalar hides. The illustrative cases make the mechanism intelligible; they complement rather than replace the aggregate experiment.

## Detail placement

Keep the categorical support, projection, and expected-value action selection in the main method. Readers need to know how a distributional Bellman target becomes a trainable loss. Move detailed proofs and all-game scores out of the narrative once their essential assumptions and conclusions are stated.

## Interpretation

The contraction result concerns fixed-policy evaluation in a Wasserstein metric; the control analysis exposes instability, and the practical loss is categorical cross-entropy. Section 6 offers several possible explanations for empirical gains. Do not collapse these into a convergence guarantee or treat return variability as calibrated epistemic uncertainty.
