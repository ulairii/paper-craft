# P02: Asynchronous Methods for Deep Reinforcement Learning

Mnih, Volodymyr; Badia, Adria Puigdomenech; Mirza, Mehdi; Graves, Alex; Lillicrap, Timothy; Harley, Tim; Silver, David; Kavukcuoglu, Koray. **ICML 2016**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/mniha16.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/mniha16.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1, 4, 5.1, 5.5–5.6 and 6; Figures 1–4; Tables 1–2; supplement 1–3.

## Writing move: Separate faster execution from better learning

A3C starts from a recognizable obstruction: correlated online updates make neural RL difficult. Parallel actors then have a scientific role, supplying diverse experience, as well as a computational role. Presenting four familiar learning algorithms under one change makes the framework's reach concrete. A resource statement such as CPU cores and training duration gives the benefit more meaning than calling the system lightweight.

## Experimental logic

The paper presents both return versus frames and return versus time. The thread sweep asks whether parallelism merely processes more experience or also changes learning efficiency. Separate sensitivity plots examine the range of successful learning rates, while additional domains test applicability.

## Detail placement

Keep hardware and the definition of the time axis beside a speed claim. Keep the selection rule beside a curve: Figure 1 uses the best five of fifty asynchronous runs, while the DQN runs use fixed hyperparameters. Full optimizer equations and task-specific network layouts can sit in the supplement.

## Interpretation

A higher mean human-normalized score does not imply the highest median. Superlinear speedups motivate an explanation but do not establish its proposed cause. The discussion explicitly leaves a useful role for replay; avoiding replay in this framework does not make replay generally unnecessary.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Parallel learning is universally more sample efficient and removes the need for replay.

After: Parallel actors reduce time to the reference score; the frame-based curves show that some one-step variants also require less experience in the tested games.
