## Writing move: Derive the architecture from properties of the input

PointNet establishes three properties before introducing its operations: points are unordered, nearby points interact, and geometric transformations matter. Symmetric aggregation, local/global feature combination, and learned alignment then answer recognizable needs. This dependency makes a simple architecture feel necessary rather than arbitrary.

## Experimental logic

The experiments revisit those properties through aggregation alternatives, alignment variants, and perturbation tests. Critical-point examples connect the theoretical discussion to the learned representation. Applications to classification and segmentation establish breadth, but the diagnostic experiments explain the design.

## Detail placement

Keep the symmetry argument and the role of alignment in the main text. The main theorem statements and intuitive explanation identify the scope of the theoretical contribution; the supplementary proofs were not read for these notes. An ordinary downstream use of max pooling need not repeat the theorem.

## Interpretation

Permutation invariance follows from the symmetric operation. Learned alignment is a different claim and does not establish exact invariance to every rigid transformation. Robustness to outliers also depends on the training setting. The discussion acknowledges a competing representation with stronger classification accuracy.
