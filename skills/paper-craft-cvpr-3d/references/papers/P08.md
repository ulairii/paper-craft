## Writing move: Use one representation to simplify several tasks

CenterPoint argues that representing an object by its center simplifies both orientation handling in detection and association in tracking. The same idea drives two tasks. Existing encoders remain explicit, which focuses the contribution on the output representation and its consequences.

## Experimental logic

Changing the head with different encoders tests the representation. Breakdowns by heading and size return to the original motivation. Crossing detector and tracker choices separates their contributions. Dense versus sparse refinement sampling asks how much extra information the second stage actually needs.

## Detail placement

Explain why box-center and surface-center features differ, and why top and bottom centers coincide in bird’s-eye view. The short geometric explanation is more useful than presenting five sample points as an unexplained convention. Put the tracking association rule next to the predicted velocity.

## Interpretation

Two-stage refinement does not improve nuScenes in the reported experiments; the paper relates this to sparse observations. A center has no intrinsic orientation, but that does not prove the full network is rotation invariant. The heading table also contains exceptions to a blanket dominance claim.
