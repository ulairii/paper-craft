## Writing move: Identify the missing interface in a cross-domain analogy

Point-BERT does not stop at proposing BERT for point clouds. It identifies the missing vocabulary and explains why individual coordinates are poor analogues of words. Local patches and a learned tokenizer create a target for masked modeling. That interface gives the analogy technical substance.

## Experimental logic

The same Transformer trained from scratch or with another pretext task distinguishes pretraining from architecture. Few-shot and real-scan transfer give representation quality different meanings. The component study separates masked modeling, patch mixing, and contrastive learning; the final system’s gains should not all be attributed to masking.

## Detail placement

Clarify the frozen tokenizer, the prediction target, and the inputs retained at masked positions. Overlapping point patches explain why random masking can make the task easier than block masking. Routine schedules can be compact, while token construction must remain visible.

## Interpretation

Transfer to ScanObjectNN includes fine-tuning; it is not zero-shot recognition. A standard Transformer backbone still receives patch embeddings with geometric design choices. Feature plots and reconstructions illustrate behavior rather than proving semantic understanding or the absence of inductive bias.
