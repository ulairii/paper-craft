## Writing move: Make an efficiency change enable a scientific capability

Point Transformer V3 places a design principle before its components: cheaper neighborhood construction can make a much larger receptive field practical. Serialization, attention, and positional encoding are presented as decisions serving this principle. The contribution is a changed scaling opportunity, not a collection of independently novel blocks.

## Experimental logic

The latency breakdown identifies expensive operations before their replacements are introduced. Tables 2–3 pair accuracy with latency for design alternatives. Table 4 varies patch size, directly testing the benefit enabled by the efficiency change. Downstream tables distinguish training from scratch and multi-dataset training.

## Detail placement

Keep the approximation in neighborhood construction explicit. Explain how changing serialization orders allows patches to exchange information. The supplementary normalization and block-structure studies support configuration choices without interrupting the main scaling argument.

## Interpretation

Patch size does not improve accuracy indefinitely: 4096 is slightly below 1024 in Table 4. The supplement also acknowledges convergence and depth-scaling limitations of dot-product attention. Receptive-field scaling, data scaling, and parameter scaling are different claims; the paper leaves broader parameter scaling for future work.
