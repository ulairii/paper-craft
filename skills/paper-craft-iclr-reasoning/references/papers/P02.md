# P02 · Least-to-Most Prompting Enables Complex Reasoning in Large Language Models

ICLR 2023 · [Paper page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=WZH7099tgfM) · [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.10625v3)

Reading coverage: main-text PDF pages 1-9; selected appendix pages 16-17,54-55. All page references use 1-based PDF page order. The following is paraphrase and analysis, not quoted source text.

## Writing lesson: A problem definition that naturally suggests the method

The easy-to-hard framing gives the reader a reason to care about decomposition before the prompting procedure is introduced. Figure 1 and Tables 1–3 make the dependency between subproblems concrete; the examples teach the idea rather than merely supply a successful output (Sections 1–3).

**Borrow this move.** Define the kind of difficulty your method addresses, then use a short example that exposes the relevant structure. Once the reader sees why later steps need earlier answers, the method's ordering feels natural.

**Original writing illustration.** “We solve each subproblem using the answers to the preceding ones” is more useful than “Our hierarchical paradigm enhances complex problem-solving.” The first sentence carries the dependency that distinguishes the method.

## Supporting reading notes

**Narrative and paragraphs.** Section 1 narrows the problem to generalization from short demonstrations to longer compositions. Section 2/Fig. 1 (p. 2) orders the method as decomposition followed by solving with previously resolved subproblems. The examples in Tables 1–3 (pp. 3–4) help define the mechanism rather than merely decorate the presentation.

**Experimental logic.** Table 4 (p. 4) slices performance by string length, and Table 12 (p. 8) by mathematical solution steps, directly testing easy-to-hard generalization. The aggregate GSM8K gap in Table 11 is modest; large SCAN gains cannot substitute for the actual results on natural mathematical problems. Appendix Section 7.3/Table 13 (p. 16) strengthens the independent CoT baseline with more examples, addressing the alternative explanation that the method simply receives more demonstrations.

**Interpretation.** High SCAN accuracy depends on task structure, demonstrations, and representation. Section 3.2 (pp. 5–6) uses both decomposition and mapping examples, along with a Python intermediate representation and postprocessing to expand answers. This does not support a claim of general reasoning without additional structure. Section 5 (p. 9) identifies cross-domain generalization of decomposition prompts as an unresolved difficulty.

**Detail placement.** The main text needs training/test complexity, demonstration budgets, subproblem dependencies, and postprocessing. Dozens of pages of complete prompts belong in the appendix, but representations and budgets that determine the conclusion do not. The small-sample error analysis in Appendix Section 7.4/Table 14 (p. 17) uses overlapping categories; its counts are not mutually exclusive probabilities. Failure examples in Section 9.5 (pp. 54–55) help discover problems but cannot estimate their population prevalence.

**Transferable recommendation.** Frame the contribution around a specific compositional generalization gap. Without cross-domain evidence, avoid claiming a general decomposition framework that solves complex reasoning.
