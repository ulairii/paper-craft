## PAV: counterintuitive claims need crossed experiments and a cost ledger

**Narrative and paragraphs.** Sections 3.3–3.4/Fig. 2 (from p. 4) distinguish a state's success probability from progress relative to a particular prover. The claim that a stronger prover need not be better is more specific than announcing a new reward. A simple example establishes the distinction before theory and experiments provide evidence within their respective domains.

**Experimental logic.** Figure 5 (p. 8) compares Q and PAV with the same search, sweeps prover strength, and crosses base models with provers. This is stronger evidence than selecting one example in which a weak model beats a strong one. Figure 4 (p. 7) examines inference cost curves, while Fig. 7 (p. 9) separately examines RL sample efficiency. Pass@N measures coverage using correctness judgments; it is not the performance of a deployed selector.

**Interpretation.** The theorem relies on assumptions including tabular natural policy gradient and oracle Q/A. It does not guarantee arbitrary search with learned LLM verifiers. Appendix H (pp. 28–29) acknowledges higher verifier data/training costs and per-batch scoring overhead, so a sample-efficiency multiplier is not a wall-time or total-FLOPs multiplier.

**Source audit.** Local wording in Section 4.1 (p. 8) uses 5–10×/10×, whereas the abstract, Fig. 4 caption, and Appendix H use 1.5–5×. This pilot retains the unresolved difference in reported efficiency wording. It neither selects the larger number nor concludes that the results are wrong. An efficiency headline requires checking the same comparison, budget, and curve.

**Detail placement.** Explain the prover's capability and role, verifier size, labeling costs, and training/inference cost accounts. The same-search controls in Appendix C (pp. 16–17) and label-coverage/precision tradeoffs in Appendix D (p. 18) should move forward if label efficiency is a central claim. Fitting errors and uncertainty about prover selection in Appendix J (pp. 29–30) constrain optimality claims.
