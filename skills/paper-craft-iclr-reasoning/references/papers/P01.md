# P01 · Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models

ICLR 2023 · [Paper page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1PL1NIMMrw) · [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.11171v4)

Reading coverage: main-text PDF pages 1-9; selected appendix pages 16-17,19. All page references use 1-based PDF page order. The following is paraphrase and analysis, not quoted source text.

## Writing lesson: A small change with a memorable intuition

The paper makes a decoding change feel consequential by giving readers a simple reason for it: multiple paths can reach the same answer. Its name describes that idea, and Fig. 1 lets the explanation move from intuition to operation before the comparisons become technical (Sections 1–2).

**Borrow this move.** Open with the bottleneck in the existing decision, explain the alternative in ordinary verbs, and let the experiments show what the small change buys. A compact mechanism does not need to be inflated into a multi-part framework.

**Original writing illustration.** Instead of “We introduce an advanced inference paradigm for enhanced reasoning,” write “We sample several reasoning paths and choose the answer they agree on most often.” The latter gives the reader an operation they can picture.

## Supporting reading notes

**Narrative and paragraphs.** The introduction acknowledges CoT's capabilities before isolating a limitation of greedy decoding: it retains only one path. Section 2/Fig. 1 (p. 2) explains the intervention as diverse paths, answer grouping, and majority selection. The contribution changes decoding and aggregation; it does not imply training a new model. Readers first need to understand why paths can differ while answers agree, before encountering the formalization.

**Experimental logic.** Section 3.2/Tables 2–3 (p. 5) establish performance across models and tasks. Figure 2 (p. 6) asks whether additional samples remain useful. Section 3.4/Fig. 3 and Table 6 (p. 7) compare sample-and-rank and beam search to distinguish generating more outputs from aggregating them and preserving diversity. The aggregation variants in Section 2/Table 1 (p. 3) test the mechanism more directly than adding more models would.

**Interpretation.** Consistency correlates with correctness (Fig. 5, p. 8), but that does not establish calibrated confidence. Section 5 (p. 9) acknowledges that intermediate reasoning can be wrong even when the final answer is correct. The supported framing concerns more reliable answer selection, not guaranteed reasoning faithfulness.

**Detail placement.** Keep the sample count, shared prompt, answer-equivalence rules, sampling settings, and compute cost in the main text. Repeated sampling statistics in Section 3.2 must not be described as independent training seeds. Full prompts and robustness to alternative prompts can go in Appendix A.1.2/Table 9 (p. 17). A Transformer tutorial is unnecessary.

**Transferable recommendation.** If the contribution only changes the selector, fix the candidate pool in the main comparison. If it also changes the candidate distribution, add a generator control. This recommendation is distilled from the experimental design; the paper does not prove that every task should use majority voting.
