## Verifier supervision: separate best achievable performance from controlled attribution

**Narrative and paragraphs.** Section 2 (p. 2) defines the object of study as verifier supervision with a fixed generator. The experiment does not train the generator through RL. The comparison between process and outcome supervision connects the data, reward models, and selected answers.

**Experimental logic.** Section 3/Fig. 3 (p. 5) shows the performance each approach can attain, while acknowledging different data and a comparison that is not apples-to-apples. Section 4/Fig. 4 (p. 6) compares process, outcome, and final-answer supervision in a smaller controlled setting. The two experiments answer different questions: what the system can achieve, and what causes the gains.

**Interpretation.** The reported 78.2% is best-of-1860 on 500 held-out MATH problems, not single-generation accuracy on the full MATH test set. Section 2.4 and Appendix C (p. 13) disclose a repartition of the original test set: 4,500 problems for training and 500 held out for testing. State that split explicitly; neither hide it nor mistakenly claim training on the final held-out answers. Section 4.2 (p. 7) does not establish stable benefits from iterative active learning, so future possibilities cannot be presented as results.

**Detail placement.** Keep the candidate count, generator, supervision/data budgets, and held-out definition in the main text. Annotation quality control appears in Appendix B (p. 13), and score aggregation in Appendix F (p. 15). If claiming robustness on long reasoning chains, the length sensitivity of a product score warrants a main-text control. The contamination discussion in Section 6.3 does not establish that contamination has been eliminated.

**Transferable recommendation.** Clearly separate best-system results from controlled experiments. If training data or generators also change, do not attribute the entire main-table difference to supervision granularity.
