## Writing move: Explain why an obvious transfer between architectures fails

QueryInst first considers attaching established mask heads to a query detector. It then uses persistent query correspondence to explain a better route for mask information: dynamic heads write into the shared queries during training and read them at prediction time.

## Experimental logic

Parallel supervision and dynamic convolution are varied independently and together. Shared versus separate queries and attention test cross-task communication. Importing HTC-style mask flow brings little extra accuracy for a large speed cost, supporting the simpler inference design.

## Detail placement

Explain the identity of a query slot across stages and where mask gradients enter it. Intermediate mask heads are used during training and removed at inference; the detector's refinement stages still run. Separate these two facts.

## Interpretation

The table reports a small additional mask gain from explicit flow, so describe it as an unfavorable tradeoff rather than literally no gain. Speed comparisons depend on query count, backbone, and hardware. Architectural reuse does not establish superiority on every instance task.
