# P04: T-Fuzz: fuzzing by program transformation

Hui Peng; Yan Shoshitaishvili; Mathias Payer. **IEEE S&P 2018**. [Paper record](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2018/program-papers.html#oakland-18-377) · [Official venue listing](https://www.ieee-security.org/TC/SP2018/program-papers.html) · [Full text](https://hexhive.epfl.ch/publications/files/18Oakland.pdf).

Source locations: Sections I–VII; Listings 1–6; Tables I–V.

## Writing move: Introduce the consequence of a new idea as part of the idea

T-Fuzz changes the object of mutation from inputs alone to inputs and programs. Its compressed-file example makes that reversal understandable. The paper immediately explains what the reversal creates: some crashes are artificial, and real faults may need different inputs in the original program. Reconstruction therefore belongs to the central argument.

## Experimental logic

The evaluation distinguishes finding candidates from reproducing faults. Ground-truth benchmarks expose the reconstruction stage’s false negatives, while real programs show practical discoveries. A stateful example explains why a genuine fault can remain unreachable along the particular reconstructed trace. This is an explanation of the method’s behavior, not merely a final limitations paragraph.

## Detail placement

Keep the approximation used to identify removable checks and the meaning of a transformed crash in the main text. Negating jumps preserves instruction addresses, which explains why trace mapping is practical. Full pseudocode for scanning and queue management can be shortened. Disclose manual analysis and imported baseline results where those affect the reported outcome.

## Interpretation

Some reported benchmark discoveries include manual confirmation after the automatic analyzer rejected them. Real-program reconstruction also encounters environment-model limitations. The contribution remains clear: changing checks creates another route to candidate discovery. It does not establish automatic reconstruction of every real fault or unrestricted superiority over input-based testing.

## Original writing illustration

Before: We bypass hard checks and automatically recover every real bug.

After: Transforming checks exposes code that input mutation has not reached; reconstruction then separates reproducible crashes from candidates requiring further analysis.

These are original illustrations, not quotations or complete descriptions of the source method.
