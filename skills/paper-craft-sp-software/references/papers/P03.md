## Writing move: Separate better observation from the policy that uses it

CollAFL explains two consequences of feedback collisions: useful seeds can disappear, and seed-selection decisions can use misleading information. It then considers the obvious alternative, a larger bitmap, before introducing its design. This makes the technical contribution necessary. Distinct instrumentation cases follow the structure of the program rather than an arbitrary module taxonomy.

## Experimental logic

The unmodified selection policy with improved feedback isolates observation quality; additional branch, descendant, and memory policies show how to use it. Long-run curves reveal changes in relative performance over time. The LAVA-M and real-program comparison with comparison-splitting instrumentation illustrates why an addition can help one workload and hurt another.

## Detail placement

Keep the known-edge qualification and bitmap-size requirement beside the collision claim. The main path metric counts retained seeds; the appendix also reports a bitmap-based metric. Explain those meanings where results appear. Full hash construction, memory-size tables, per-program curves, and the CVE inventory can remain supporting material.

## Interpretation

Collision-free identifiers for known edges do not represent complete ordered execution paths. The 200-hour campaign is one trial; the repeated experiments use a different, shorter setup. Manual triage distinguishes 157 vulnerabilities from the 134 previously unknown to vendors and 95 assigned CVEs. These distinctions support a precise discovery story.
