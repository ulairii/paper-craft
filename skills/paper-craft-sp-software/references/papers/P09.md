## Writing move: Choose experiments that explain the real objective

SAVIOR separates two problems: reaching code with potential faults and satisfying a fault condition once there. Different examples motivate prioritization and fault-directed solving, and the evaluation returns to both. The resulting argument explains why the method matters even without consistently faster basic-block coverage.

## Experimental logic

Paired coverage and violation curves make that distinction visible. Applying the fault-solving stage to other fuzzers’ saved inputs demonstrates a reusable benefit. Triage then translates sanitizer reports into program consequences. Cases of an out-of-bounds access, an incorrect result, and harmless overflow teach the reader how to interpret the discovery count.

## Detail placement

Keep the supported fault families, source access, path-specific reasoning, and baseline construction in the main text. Driller is reimplemented on the authors’ executor; the comparison is not with its stock implementation. The additional offline pass consumes extra work. Detailed LLVM instrumentation and full label tables fit in the appendix.

## Interpretation

Potential labels, triggered violations, impactful defects, confirmed reports, and exploitable cases are different quantities. The paper reports 481 violations and classifies 243 as impactful, with 16 developer confirmations. Speed figures should not be reused as endpoint count gains. Borrow the objective-centered story while stating the metric that the supplied evidence actually measures.
