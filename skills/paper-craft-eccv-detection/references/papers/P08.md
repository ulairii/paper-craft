# P08: Open-Vocabulary DETR with Conditional Matching

Yuhang Zang and Wei Li and Kaiyang Zhou and Chen Huang and Chen Change Loy. **ECCV 2022**. [Official paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/1248_ECCV_2022_paper.php) · [Full text](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136690107.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 3.2–3.4 and 4.1–4.5; Tables 2–5; supplement B–D.

## Writing move: Change the training question so unlabeled concepts can participate

OV-DETR replaces closed-set classification with conditional matchability: does a predicted region match this text or image query? The image-query route lets a region supply a condition even when its class name is unavailable. This makes the training objective the central contribution rather than presenting CLIP as a universal plug-in.

## Experimental logic

The simple CLIP-classifier baseline transfers somewhat to novel classes, but directly adding unlabeled proposals hurts. Conditional matching then makes those proposals useful. This failed intermediate baseline is a strong explanatory move. Separate base/novel results, cross-dataset transfer, and image-query examples answer different generalization questions.

## Detail placement

Define the condition, the binary target, and how query copies handle multiple instances. The supplement identifies a base-trained Deformable DETR as the proposal source and reports similar novel-class proposal recall to the ViLD source. The lesson is how proposals are used, not simply better initial coverage. LVIS uses mask AP and the COCO setting uses box AP50. Those metrics are not interchangeable.

## Interpretation

The conditional design makes inference cost grow with the vocabulary; sharing the image representation does not remove repeated decoder work. Present flexibility for text and exemplar queries with that cost. Open-vocabulary transfer depends on pretrained vision-language information, not absence of all exposure to novel concepts.
