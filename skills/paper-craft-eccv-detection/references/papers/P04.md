# P04: Probabilistic Anchor Assignment with IoU Prediction for Object Detection

Kang Kim and Hee Seok Lee. **ECCV 2020**. [Official paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/5041_ECCV_2020_paper.php) · [Full text](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123700358.pdf).

## Writing move: Connect assignment, learning, and ranking through one quality notion

PAA observes that overlap alone does not describe what the current model can recognize and localize. It fits a two-component mixture to model-dependent anchor scores, then extends the same classification–localization reasoning to test-time ranking through predicted IoU. A shared quality notion gives the assignment and ranking changes a common purpose.

## Experimental logic

Fixed positive counts and fixed score thresholds test simpler alternatives to adaptive separation. The latter can fail early when scores are low, making the need for adaptation concrete. IoU prediction versus centerness, and the separate score-voting increment, explain which part of the final result comes from each change.

## Detail placement

Keep the construction of the score and its relation to losses. State that the mixture is fitted to candidate scores and that candidates are preselected. Probabilistic assignment does not eliminate every design choice. The IoU head and NMS ranking belong in the inference description; EM belongs to training.

## Interpretation

A fitted mixture supplies an assignment rule, not guaranteed calibrated probabilities that an anchor is truly positive. The useful claim is adaptive selection under the tested setup. IoU prediction's improvement over centerness here does not make every geometric centerness prior invalid.
