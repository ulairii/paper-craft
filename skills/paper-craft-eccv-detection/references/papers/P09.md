## Writing move: Use a common design map to explain a multi-part system

Grounding DINO maps language fusion onto three stages of a detector: feature enhancement, query selection, and decoding. This map makes its components parts of one design principle. Sub-sentence masking then addresses a concrete training-input issue: unrelated category names should not influence each other's text representation.

## Experimental logic

Matched-data comparisons distinguish the architecture from expanded pretraining. Fusion ablations test the design map, while COCO, LVIS, ODinW, and referring-expression results probe different forms of transfer. Adding referring data helps some settings and hurts others, making data composition part of the scientific explanation rather than a one-dimensional more-data story.

## Detail placement

Define zero-shot as not training on the target dataset's training split; overlapping concepts in other data remain possible. Keep pretraining sources and fine-tuning status beside results. Explain what language conditions query selection and which text interactions are masked. Move routine feature dimensions behind that account. The supplement distinguishes caption pseudo-labels and the Objects365 versions used for the two model sizes.

## Interpretation

Recognizing a category does not ensure understanding a detailed referring expression: the paper reports weak referring performance without relevant training. Rare-category results also expose a boundary of the overall gain. Mean and median across datasets describe different summaries; they alone do not establish a variance claim.
