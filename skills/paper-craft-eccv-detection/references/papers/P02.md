Nicolas Carion and Francisco Massa and Gabriel Synnaeve and Nicolas Usunier and Alexander Kirillov and Sergey Zagoruyko. **ECCV 2020**. [Official paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/html/832_ECCV_2020_paper.php) · [Full text](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2020/papers_ECCV/papers/123460205.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1, 3.1–3.2, and 4.1–4.3; Tables 1–2 and Figure 4.

## Writing move: Make a familiar task's output structure the organizing principle

DETR starts from the fact that detection returns a set. Bipartite matching and parallel transformer decoding are introduced as complementary ingredients for predicting that set directly. The contribution is a simpler formulation that becomes competitive with a strong detector, rather than a small isolated leaderboard improvement.

## Experimental logic

Strengthening Faster R-CNN with a longer schedule, crop augmentation, and an overlap loss makes the comparison persuasive. Decoder-stage predictions with and without NMS explain how duplicate handling develops through the architecture. Large- versus small-object results refine the story: comparable overall AP arises from a substantial large-object advantage alongside a small-object deficit.

## Detail placement

Explain unique assignment, unused prediction slots, and the difference between matching cost and training loss. Auxiliary decoder losses and the long training schedule affect how the formulation succeeds and deserve concise visibility. The panoptic extension is a separate demonstration with its own mask head and output procedure.

## Interpretation

Competitive performance can validate a useful reformulation without universal superiority. The authors acknowledge difficult optimization and small objects early. Attention patterns offer a plausible explanation for global-context benefits, not a proof of the sole cause. The panoptic gain also does not imply better instance mask AP.
