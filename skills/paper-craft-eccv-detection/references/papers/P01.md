## Writing move: Make a new output representation solve a visible problem

CornerNet replaces enumerated anchor boxes with paired corners. The introduction then exposes the difficulty created by this choice: a box corner may have no local object evidence. Corner pooling follows directly by looking along the two boundary directions. This gives the representation and its supporting operator one coherent story.

## Experimental logic

The pooling comparison keeps parameter count matched. Larger gains on medium and large objects connect the result to longer distances between corners and visible boundaries. Fixed versus object-dependent penalty radii test a second geometric choice. Ground-truth heatmap and offset substitutions identify corner detection as a major bottleneck, rather than treating the final AP as the only result.

## Detail placement

Keep corner pairing, the role of embedding distance, and the offset correction for quantization. Explain why nearby negative locations receive reduced penalties. Move the hourglass layer inventory behind this account. State that anchor-free prediction still uses heatmap suppression, pairing rules, and Soft-NMS.

## Interpretation

The strongest reported AP uses multi-scale evaluation. Removing anchor design is not the same as removing all post-processing or becoming a real-time detector. The object-size pattern supports the pooling rationale without establishing that corners are always easier than centers.
