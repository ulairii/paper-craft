# P01: CommanderSong: A Systematic Approach for Practical Adversarial Voice Recognition

Xuejing Yuan; Yuxuan Chen; Yue Zhao; Yunhui Long; Xiaokang Liu; Kai Chen; Shengzhi Zhang; Heqing Huang; Xiaofeng Wang; Carl A. Gunter. **USENIX Security 2018**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/yuan-xuejing) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-yuan.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–7; digital and over-the-air evaluation, perception study and delivery cases; appendix.

## Writing move: Make each practical obstacle generate a design and an experiment

CommanderSong develops its argument through three practical questions: surviving physical playback, concealing command meaning in audio, and reaching a recognizer through delivery channels. These questions make the design choices intelligible. The move from device-specific noise to a more general noise model addresses the first obstacle; songs address the second. The reader sees why each ingredient exists before encountering the evaluation.

## Experimental logic

Digital transcription and over-the-air transcription establish different capabilities. The perception study adds another dimension: recognizing abnormal audio is different from understanding its embedded command. Comparing speakers and delivery paths extends the demonstrated setting, while the tradeoff between noise tolerance and audible distortion explains why practical success is not a single number.

## Detail placement

Keep the recognizer, playback path, distance, success metric, and perception question beside the corresponding result. The pseudo voice-control demonstration uses recorded audio with Kaldi; do not let a diagram imply that commercial devices executed commands. Full song lists and secondary delivery cases can follow the main experiment. Explain the stronger conditions required by jointly optimized recognizers.

## Interpretation

The reported word-recognition rate is not a command-execution rate. Participants could notice artifacts without recognizing command words. Direct transfer to DeepSpeech failed before further optimization. These distinctions make the practical contribution more understandable: several obstacles are addressed, but each has its own measured outcome.
