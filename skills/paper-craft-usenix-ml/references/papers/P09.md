## Writing move: Explain why a simple construction exploits the learning objective

PoisonedEncoder locates the attack in unlabeled pretraining while leaving downstream training intact. The key explanation is simple: two crops of one combined image can show a target and a reference image, so ordinary contrastive training encourages the relation the attacker wants. The training objective makes the construction persuasive; complicated notation is not what makes it novel.

## Experimental logic

The comparison reports both targeted success and ordinary downstream accuracy. Feature similarity connects the attack objective to its outcome, while combination-method experiments explain the benefit of varied layouts. Destination-class differences give the results an interpretation beyond a mean success rate. Removing random cropping tests both the proposed attack mechanism and the utility cost of removing it.

## Detail placement

Keep injection access, reference images, the chosen target, and the poisoning budget in the main text. Multiple-task experiments preserve the budget per task, so do not imply the same total budget. Explain the construction as a heuristic motivated by the bilevel objective. Generic contrastive-learning derivations and routine training settings can be supporting material.

## Interpretation

The study injects images into controlled datasets; it does not measure successful collection from a public crawl. Arbitrary target selection does not imply perfect success for every target. Clean-data fine-tuning reduces attack success without the same utility penalty but requires clean data. The defense story is about costs and resources, not five universally ineffective defenses.
