# P02: AttriGuard: A Practical Defense Against Attribute Inference Attacks via Adversarial Machine Learning

Jinyuan Jia; Neil Zhenqiang Gong. **USENIX Security 2018**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity18/presentation/jia-jinyuan) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity18/sec18-jia.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–7; two-phase optimization, utility evaluation and robust inference experiments; Appendix A.

## Writing move: Turn a familiar attack mechanism into a constructive privacy idea

AttriGuard reverses the usual role of adversarial examples: instead of corrupting a useful prediction, a user changes public data to frustrate unwanted attribute inference. The high-dimensional search is decomposed into candidate perturbations and a probability distribution over those candidates. This decomposition gives a simple account of what the method chooses and why uncertainty about the attacker matters.

## Experimental logic

Attack accuracy and recommendation quality answer the two halves of the problem. The study compares defenses at similar inference accuracy, then examines recommendation utility. Varying attacker training overlap and model architecture tests whether the surrogate-based construction remains useful. The discrete rating alphabet explains why an otherwise strong continuous attack can be a poor candidate generator.

## Detail placement

Keep the allowed rating changes, expected modification budget, attacker information, and target output distribution in the main text. An expected number of changes is not a per-user cap. Explain fallback behavior when a restricted policy cannot produce a candidate. Put the full optimization derivation after the operational account of candidate generation and randomization.

## Interpretation

The convex mixture problem is solved conditional on generated candidates; this does not establish a globally minimal perturbation. Accuracy below a majority-class baseline does not stop an attacker from ignoring the released data. Differential privacy and attribute inference have different objectives, so a task-specific utility advantage is not a general ranking of privacy definitions.
