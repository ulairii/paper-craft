# P05: Local Model Poisoning Attacks to Byzantine-Robust Federated Learning

Minghong Fang; Xiaoyu Cao; Jinyuan Jia; Neil Gong. **USENIX Security 2020**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity20/presentation/fang) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec20summer_fang_prepub.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–6; attack objectives, knowledge settings, aggregation comparisons and defense evaluation; Appendices A–C.

## Writing move: Expose a gap between a guarantee and the operational question

Local Model Poisoning asks whether aggregation rules that tolerate Byzantine behavior still protect finite training runs from deliberate accuracy degradation. It locates the attacker in the learning process, where a compromised client can submit a crafted update. A directional displacement objective makes the attack concrete before aggregator-specific constructions explain how the update can survive selection.

## Experimental logic

Full and partial knowledge separate two attacker capabilities. Aggregation-specific results and transfer between rules show why the mechanism depends on the aggregation decision. Varying client heterogeneity explains when benign updates themselves make separation difficult. Defense experiments then ask whether performance-based filtering can repair the observed failures.

## Detail placement

Keep the number of compromised clients, attacker knowledge, aggregation rule, and finite training budget in the main text. State that the attack aims at general test-error degradation rather than a trigger-specific backdoor. Put long bounds and constructions for secondary aggregation rules in the appendix. Mark oracle selection of a favorable defender iteration when using those results.

## Interpretation

The experiments do not refute a theorem outside its assumptions. The restricted directional objective is an approximation, not a global maximizer of model error. Transfer is asymmetric, and the directional variant does not win every comparison. These differences help explain how the aggregation rule creates the opportunity instead of weakening the entire story.
