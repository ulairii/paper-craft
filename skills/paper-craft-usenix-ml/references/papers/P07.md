## Writing move: Make the interaction between standard ingredients carry the contribution

FLAME explains its three components through the malicious update each addresses: directional outliers, scaled updates, and residual stealthy updates. The central claim is their interaction. Filtering and clipping reduce how much noise is needed, allowing the final component to suppress remaining attack behavior at a lower utility cost. This is stronger exposition than simply naming three modules.

## Experimental logic

The noise-level comparison shows the changing security–utility tradeoff as components are added. The naive-combination experiment asks whether stacking familiar ingredients is enough. Model-alignment attacks demonstrate why perfect filtering is not the objective: the complete method can suppress an attack that the filtering stage misses. Main-task and backdoor accuracy remain paired.

## Detail placement

Keep the honest-majority condition, access to individual updates, adaptive clipping rule, and attack family in the main text. Explain that deep-model noise calibration uses approximations and empirical support. Move detailed clustering steps to the appendix. If discussing private FLAME, state its two non-colluding servers, clustering approximation, and substantial runtime cost.

## Interpretation

Zero backdoor accuracy in some settings is not elimination of every possible backdoor. The edge-case and PGD experiments have residual attack success and main-task losses. The large reported privacy parameters do not justify a strong privacy claim for ordinary FLAME. Borrow the interaction argument while expressing theoretical and empirical support at their actual scopes.
