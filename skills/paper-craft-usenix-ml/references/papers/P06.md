# P06: PatchGuard: A Provably Robust Defense against Adversarial Patches via Small Receptive Fields and Masking

Chong Xiang; Arjun Nitin Bhagoji; Vikash Sehwag; Prateek Mittal. **USENIX Security 2021**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity21/presentation/xiang) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec21-xiang.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–8; feature-space diagnosis, robust masking, Tables 4–14; Appendices A–F.

## Writing move: Map two observed failure causes to two complementary design choices

PatchGuard identifies two sources of patch vulnerability: large receptive fields spread corruption, and ordinary aggregation lets a few large feature values dominate. Small receptive fields address the first; masking addresses the second. The resulting adversary dilemma—large evidence attracts masking, small evidence has limited influence—introduces the proof through a comprehensible mechanism.

## Experimental logic

Clean and certified accuracy establish the main tradeoff. Clipping, masking, receptive-field size, and feature type then explain the operating point. The training comparison in Appendix A shows that provable adversarial training also contributes substantially to the BagNet results. Conservative mask sizes test uncertainty about the patch bound rather than only the best-matched setting.

## Detail placement

Keep the single contiguous patch, size bound, arbitrary location, architecture, and meaning of certification in the main text. Distinguish the cost of adopting a small-receptive-field architecture from the incremental cost of masking. The certification intuition belongs beside the method; detailed receptive-field derivations and additional top-k analysis fit in the appendix.

## Interpretation

ImageNette and ImageNet have different certified accuracies. A small drop from BagNet does not imply a small drop from an undefended ResNet. Masking every clean image is not the same as misclassifying every clean image. The published multi-patch discussion proposes extensions rather than demonstrating the main guarantee unchanged in that setting.
