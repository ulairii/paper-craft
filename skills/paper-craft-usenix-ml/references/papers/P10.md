# P10: ML-Doctor: Holistic Risk Assessment of Inference Attacks Against Machine Learning Models

Yugeng Liu; Rui Wen; Xinlei He; Ahmed Salem; Zhikun Zhang; Michael Backes; Emiliano De Cristofaro; Mario Fritz; Yang Zhang. **USENIX Security 2022**. [Official paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity22/presentation/liu-yugeng) · [Full text](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/sec22-liu-yugeng.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–9; threat taxonomy, three research questions, cross-attack analysis and defenses; Appendix A.

## Writing move: Make a common comparison reveal a relationship isolated studies miss

ML-Doctor makes comparison itself the contribution. A taxonomy of model access and auxiliary data establishes which attacks can be compared. The experiments then answer three questions about datasets, overfitting, and relationships between attacks. The reusable software enables this analysis, but the central story is what the aligned comparison reveals about different risks.

## Experimental logic

The same target models support membership inference, inversion, attribute inference, and extraction experiments. The negative association between membership inference and model-stealing performance is followed by training-epoch analysis. Defense experiments show why a change beneficial for one measured risk can leave another unchanged or increase it. Alternate metrics in the appendix extend the main comparisons.

## Detail placement

Keep each attack’s information, output, and metric explicit: agreement measures behavioral extraction, whereas class-reconstruction quality is not exact training-record recovery. State the image-classification scope and the different architectures used for DP-SGD and distillation. Put the full attack-network configurations and repeated metric grids after the main results.

## Interpretation

The dataset-complexity ordering is qualitative and changes several factors at once. Cross-model correlations do not establish a universal causal security tradeoff. Partial and shadow datasets also differ in size. DP-SGD and distillation are not evaluated as a matched architectural comparison, and some utility drops are substantial. The robust writing lesson is to explain differentiated risks rather than announce one overall security score.
