Fujimoto, Scott; Gu, Shixiang (Shane). **NeurIPS 2021**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/a8166da05c5a094f7dc03724b41886e5-Abstract.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/a8166da05c5a094f7dc03724b41886e5-Paper.pdf).

## Writing move: Sell simplicity through a controlled quality–complexity comparison

A Minimalist Approach to Offline Reinforcement Learning asks how much machinery is necessary for competitive performance. Adding behavior cloning and state normalization to TD3 gives the paper a concise center. It credits prior combinations of RL and imitation, and makes the value of an accessible baseline the contribution rather than inventing novelty in every component.

## Experimental logic

Return comparisons and measured training times jointly establish the practical argument. Ablations show that policy improvement and behavior regularization are both useful, while normalization has a smaller role. Hyperparameter sweeps reveal a workable region. Supplementary difficult datasets make the method’s scope clearer, including weak performance on larger mazes.

## Detail placement

Keep the Q-scaled actor objective and the meaning of its coefficient in the method. Explain tuning on designated datasets and whether timing excludes evaluation. Dataset versions and baseline reruns affect comparisons. Full optimizer settings and secondary transfer experiments fit in the appendix.

## Interpretation

Minimal changes do not mean no hyperparameters or solved offline instability. State normalization is removed for the AntMaze experiments because it hurts there. Runtime comparisons depend on the actual number of training updates; a long hypothetical Transformer run is not its standard training cost. The benefit can be easier use and lower cost without a new application frontier.
