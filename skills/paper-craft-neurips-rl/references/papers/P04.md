## Writing move: Make the meaning of conservatism precise before presenting the objective

Conservative Q-Learning turns out-of-distribution value optimism into a concrete learning objective. The exposition distinguishes lowering every action value from learning a lower estimate of a policy’s expected value. This distinction gives the data-value maximization term a reason to exist and makes the method more specific than a general appeal to caution.

## Experimental logic

Offline benchmark results establish usefulness across data regimes. Predicted-value gaps and objective variants ask whether the proposed pessimism is doing the intended job. Mixed-quality datasets and difficult manipulation tasks reveal where the method helps most. Those analyses connect the objective to behavior rather than treating a return table as an explanation.

## Detail placement

Keep both regularization terms, their sampling distributions, and the expected-value meaning of the bound in the main text. Explain the assumptions separating tabular or local approximation analysis from neural experiments. The practical coefficient selection and action-sampling approximation matter when explaining the implemented objective; exhaustive settings can follow.

## Interpretation

A lower-bound argument under stated conditions is not a guarantee of safe deployment. A larger ratio of normalized benchmark scores is not the same ratio of raw return. Small nonzero performance on a difficult task should not be called a complete solution. The strongest story is improved offline learning through a targeted correction to value estimation.
