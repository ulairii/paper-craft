## Writing move: Let a mismatch between theory and practice lead to a useful design

MBPO asks when a learned model is useful for policy optimization. A pessimistic error bound alone favors avoiding model rollouts; empirical model generalization motivates a less pessimistic account and short branched rollouts. This progression makes the practical method understandable without pretending that every implementation choice follows directly from a theorem.

## Experimental logic

The central comparison is return versus real environment interaction. Rollout-length experiments then explain why short synthetic branches work well. A high-update-ratio control without model data distinguishes synthetic experience from simply doing more optimization. Long-horizon prediction quality and policy performance need not move together, which makes the analysis more informative than a single model-error score.

## Detail placement

Keep the source of rollout starting states, rollout horizon, real/synthetic mixture, and underlying policy optimizer in the method. Distinguish the environment episode length from model rollout length. Explain which error estimate is empirical and which quantity appears in a bound. Architecture inventories can remain supplementary.

## Interpretation

Interaction efficiency is not the same as wall-clock acceleration. A model can predict reasonably over a long horizon while those rollouts harm policy learning. The practical SAC updates do not exactly solve the idealized policy optimization in the analysis. Short rollouts provide a strong tested design, not a universal optimal horizon.
