## Writing move: Make a benchmark useful through the decisions it enables

RL Unplugged builds its story around obstacles to meaningful offline-RL comparison: diverse observations and actions, difficult data distributions, and policy selection without unrestricted online access. The contribution is a suite and an evaluation protocol. A domain matrix explains coverage, while empirical findings show why the suite changes what researchers can learn.

## Experimental logic

Online and offline selection settings make model selection part of the scientific question. Behavior cloning is strong in some difficult locomotion settings and weak in others, demonstrating that benchmark breadth matters. Transferring hyperparameters from designated selection tasks is a specific offline-selection baseline, not a new universal off-policy evaluation method.

## Detail placement

Keep data-generation policies, selection-task access, and score normalization in the main protocol. Explain exclusions and success downsampling because they shape dataset difficulty. The Atari reference is the reported DQN baseline rather than human performance. Full task configurations and trajectory construction details belong in supporting material.

## Interpretation

Weak results from a particular baseline implementation do not prove that the algorithm cannot work. Different task sets under different selection protocols do not isolate selection as the only cause of score differences. Describing an available dataset is distinct from reporting a complete baseline evaluation on it. A benchmark paper can contribute through revealing heterogeneous behavior.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our benchmark proves that existing offline algorithms do not work.

After: The suite exposes substantial variation across tasks and selection protocols, providing reusable data and comparisons that make offline-RL progress easier to interpret.
