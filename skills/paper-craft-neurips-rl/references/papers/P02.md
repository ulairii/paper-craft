Christiano, Paul F; Leike, Jan; Brown, Tom; Martic, Miljan; Legg, Shane; Amodei, Dario. **NeurIPS 2017**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/d5e2c0adad503c91f91df240d0cd4e49-Abstract.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/file/d5e2c0adad503c91f91df240d0cd4e49-Paper.pdf).

## Writing move: Make the expensive resource define the contribution

Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences makes reward specification the practical obstacle. Short trajectory comparisons supply an accessible human interface, and a learned reward model scales that feedback across many environment interactions. The paper explains the preference model and policy learner as interacting roles. The story concerns useful behavior from limited human effort, not a new solution to every aspect of alignment.

## Experimental logic

Known hidden rewards permit quantitative evaluation; novel behaviors demonstrate what fixed benchmark rewards cannot express. Synthetic preferences and real human labels answer different questions. Clip-length experiments reveal a resource tradeoff: longer clips contain more information per comparison, but not necessarily per unit of human time. Feedback-loop ablations show why continuing to query can matter.

## Detail placement

Keep the query interface, label budget, clip duration, and distinction between human and synthetic labels visible. Explain which game signals are hidden or removed. Report the smaller number of real-human runs honestly. Rater instructions and full optimization settings fit in the appendix once the feedback loop is clear.

## Interpretation

Few comparisons relative to environment steps means label efficiency, not an equally large reduction in environment interactions. Novel behaviors include qualitative demonstrations and author raters. Reward-model exploitation motivates ongoing feedback in these experiments; it does not prove all offline preference learning is impossible. Query disagreement is a heuristic whose benefit varies.
