## Writing move: Explain why combining existing ideas requires a new design choice

COMBO combines model-based data augmentation with conservative value learning by identifying where the penalty should act. Synthetic states receive pessimistic treatment while observed data support value maximization. The story targets the need for explicit uncertainty penalties, rather than claiming to remove every model assumption. A direct combination of CQL and MBPO provides the natural alternative.

## Experimental logic

Generalization tasks come before standard benchmark comparisons, aligning evidence with the contribution. Reward-relabeled tasks ask whether a model helps learn behavior different from the data-collection policy. The supplementary CQL-plus-MBPO comparison tests the state-distribution choice directly. Pixel observations then extend the practical setting, and offline parameter-selection examples support a separate usability claim.

## Detail placement

Keep the penalty distribution distinct from the Bellman-update mixture. Explain which practical sampling distributions differ from the ideal analysis. Reward relabeling requires access to the new reward definition. State that parameter selection searches specified candidates, with other settings inherited from prior work. Full model architectures and grids can be supplementary.

## Interpretation

The expected-value and policy-improvement arguments have finite-space and error assumptions; they do not guarantee safe neural deployment. Poor correlation for tested uncertainty heuristics does not invalidate all uncertainty estimation. A learned probabilistic ensemble remains part of the method. The direct-combination comparison supports a targeted design contribution rather than mere assembly.
