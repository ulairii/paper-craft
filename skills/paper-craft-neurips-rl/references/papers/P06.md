## Writing move: Give a familiar architecture a new problem formulation

Decision Transformer makes the formulation central: predict actions from a trajectory context and desired return. The reader first understands what is conditioned on and what is generated, then sees the Transformer implementation. The paper connects supervised sequence modeling to decision-making while crediting earlier return-conditioned approaches.

## Experimental logic

Offline returns establish viability. Percentile behavior cloning asks whether simply retaining strong trajectories explains the result. Desired-return and context experiments investigate conditioning, while delayed rewards test a setting in which episode-level outcomes remain available despite changed reward timing. A separate graph construction explores trajectory stitching with additional modeling choices.

## Detail placement

Keep token order, action-only training loss, target-return updates, and context length visible. Explain whether the behavior-cloning comparison shares an architecture. Separate the main policy from the graph experiment’s joint return model and prior. Complete model settings can remain supplementary.

## Interpretation

Desired return is an input, not a guaranteed achieved return. Strong delayed-reward performance is not unique proof of attention-based credit assignment; filtered behavior cloning also benefits. Attention patterns illustrate associations rather than establish causality. The separate graph result should not be presented as a general stitching guarantee for the main model.
