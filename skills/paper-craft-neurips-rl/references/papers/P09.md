## Writing move: Make an empirical analysis valuable through the conclusions it changes

The Statistical Precipice paper starts with a scientific question: would the same experiment repeated with a few different runs lead to the same conclusion? A large empirical case study makes the problem tangible before statistical tools are introduced. Reanalyses then show how those tools change the interpretation of progress. This is a narrative for an analysis contribution, not a requirement to turn every paper into a statistical methods paper.

## Experimental logic

High-run experiments provide empirical reference distributions. Controlled protocol changes distinguish evaluation choices from algorithm changes. Performance profiles reveal where methods help across tasks, and robust aggregates reduce the influence of extreme scores. The important result is sometimes a changed ordering or unresolved comparison rather than a new best-performing agent.

## Detail placement

Explain what is averaged and what is resampled. The interquartile mean pools task–run scores, while run-stratified bootstrap intervals condition on the chosen tasks. Probability of improvement concerns sampled task/run comparisons. Put complete statistical definitions in supporting material after explaining the question each display answers.

## Interpretation

A finite high-run reference is not exact population truth. Confidence intervals cannot create information absent from a few runs, and overlapping separate intervals are not a formal no-effect result. Probability of improvement is not a posterior probability that one algorithm is universally better. The lesson is to make uncertainty informative in the scientific story.

## Original writing illustration

Before: Our statistics prove that no recent algorithm improves reinforcement learning.

After: Repeated-run analyses show that several apparent improvements are sensitive to aggregation and evaluation choices; distributional summaries make the supported comparisons clearer.

These are original illustrations, not quotations or complete descriptions of the source method.
