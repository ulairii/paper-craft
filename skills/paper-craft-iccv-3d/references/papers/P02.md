## Writing move: Learn the uncertain relation and retain the solved geometry

Deep Closest Point decomposes registration into representation, correspondence, and rigid alignment. It learns the ambiguous matching problem while retaining an analytic SVD solution for the rigid transform. This is a stronger contribution story than describing an undifferentiated end-to-end network: the reader understands why one part should learn and another should remain geometric.

## Experimental logic

The SVD-versus-MLP comparison supports the analytic head. The feature and attention combinations expose an interaction: attention improves the DGCNN configuration but does not improve every PointNet result. Synthetic shape splits, unseen-category splits, and noise experiments answer different generalization questions. ICP refinement shows that a classical solver can remain useful after a learned initialization.

## Detail placement

Explain the soft correspondence matrix and the weighted target points before the SVD. State the transformation and observation setting near the first result: these experiments align synthetically transformed shape samples, not arbitrary partially overlapping real scans. Routine network dimensions need less space than that distinction.

## Interpretation

Describe generalization to the tested categories and perturbations. Do not infer full SLAM robustness, partial-overlap registration, or universal speed superiority. A positive aggregate result can coexist with a competitor's better error statistic in a particular split.
