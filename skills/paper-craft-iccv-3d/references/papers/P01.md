## Writing move: Move evidence to where the prediction is defined

VoteNet begins with a geometric mismatch: observed points lie on surfaces, while box centers often lie in empty space. Voting is therefore a way to gather evidence near the prediction target. This makes the method feel necessary before the reader sees its modules. The connection to classical Hough voting explains what is retained and what learning changes.

## Experimental logic

BoxNet keeps the backbone and a comparable proposal module but aggregates seeds instead of votes. Its objectness labels are adapted to surface seeds; simply deleting the voting loss would not create the intended baseline. Context and aggregation-radius experiments then explain when the relocated evidence is useful. The relationship between category gains and surface-to-center distance is supporting correlation, not a causal proof.

## Detail placement

Keep the meaning of seed coordinates, vote offsets, and vote features in the method. Explain the BoxNet label adaptation beside its comparison. Put layer widths and box-loss weights in the appendix. The height feature deserves a brief input description: geometry-only does not mean raw XYZ alone.

## Interpretation

The paper's geometry-based success coexists with difficulty on thin, texture-dependent objects. Use that boundary to explain the representation's strengths. Do not turn it into a claim that RGB is unnecessary for all categories or that the method is faster than every baseline.
