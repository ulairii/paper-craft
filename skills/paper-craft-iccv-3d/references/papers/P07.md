## Writing move: Explain why an underused representation needs different operations

RangeDet defends native LiDAR range images by identifying operations that do not transfer cleanly from ordinary image detection. Range changes apparent object size, and neighboring image cells have different metric relationships. Range-conditioned assignment, geometric kernels, and proposal aggregation answer these concrete mismatches rather than forming an arbitrary module list.

## Experimental logic

Range-versus-area assignment controls the number of assigned examples per level. Cartesian and range differences are compared with pixel differences to test what geometry contributes. Ordered concatenation is compared with pooling because range grids have meaningful order. Weighted NMS is also tested on other representations, showing that an existing technique can be especially useful in a new setting.

## Detail placement

Keep the native single-view sensor assumption and the correspondence between image cells and 3D measurements. Coordinate-aware rotation and occlusion-aware copy-paste deserve explanation because they preserve the representation's meaning. Routine augmentation probabilities are less central. Label training subsets and BEV versus 3D metrics where results are introduced.

## Interpretation

The gains explain the competitiveness of range-view detection, not its superiority for every class and sensor arrangement. A merged cloud is not automatically losslessly representable as one native range image. Discuss distance-dependent outcomes as evidence about the chosen geometry.
