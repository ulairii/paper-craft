## Writing move: Make the benchmark construction explain the scientific gap

ScanObjectNN questions whether high accuracy on clean CAD shapes captures recognition in real scans. Background clutter, missing surfaces, and object perturbations become explicit dataset variants. The benchmark construction carries the argument: it turns a broad realism concern into observable conditions, then introduces a background-aware baseline for one of them.

## Experimental logic

Separate foreground-only scans, scans with background, and controlled perturbations. Cross-dataset evaluation uses shared categories and removes background to focus that comparison on other distribution differences. Different train/test scenes avoid making scene familiarity the task. The proposed joint classification and foreground-mask model addresses background handling rather than claiming to solve every identified gap.

## Detail placement

Distinguish original objects from augmented samples. Explain what each perturbation changes and which object-retention condition keeps it meaningful. Foreground-mask supervision is essential to understanding the proposed baseline. A lengthy inventory of acquisition parameters is less useful in the opening than a concrete CAD-to-scan failure.

## Interpretation

Background can be useful context as well as distraction; not every model loses accuracy when it is present. Explain the observed pattern rather than insisting on one universal effect. A mask predicted incorrectly on clean CAD data also makes the learned assumption visible.
