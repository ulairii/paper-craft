## Writing move: Translate a general operator into the structure of the data

Point Transformer motivates attention through point sets, then makes the specific design legible: local neighborhoods, vector weights, and relative position in both weight and value branches. The narrative moves from an operator to a residual block, a hierarchy, and task heads. This ordering lets the reader understand the contribution before the full architecture.

## Experimental logic

Compare local MLP and pooling alternatives with scalar and vector attention, then examine position encoding and neighborhood size. These experiments explain the operator, not merely the final network score. The neighborhood sweep has an interior optimum; larger context is not automatically better in this configuration.

## Detail placement

Keep the distinction between per-point permutation equivariance and pooled invariance precise. Supporting variable point counts is not a guarantee that adding or removing observations leaves predictions unchanged. Relative coordinates also do not, by themselves, establish rotation invariance. Layer lists can follow the conceptual definition.

## Interpretation

Use the metric that supports the sentence: the best instance-averaged part IoU does not imply the best category-averaged score. Parameter count alone is not runtime. The writing lesson is to identify the useful design choices without expanding them into universal properties of attention.
