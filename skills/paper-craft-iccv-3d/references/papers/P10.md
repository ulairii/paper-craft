## Writing move: Preserve the useful functions of a costly representation

SparseBEV keeps queries sparse while making context, image sampling, and feature mixing adaptive. Each operation restores a useful function often associated with dense BEV features. The story is therefore about what a sparse detector must still do, not a blanket rejection of dense processing: its image backbone remains dense.

## Experimental logic

Scale adaptation, ego/object motion alignment, and static/adaptive mixing comparisons answer different questions. Ego alignment explains most of the alignment gain; object velocity adds an incremental benefit. The dual-branch supplement compares temporal resolution and image resolution, including an extra-feature-level alternative. Its experiments use a different implementation setting from the main paper, which the authors state explicitly.

## Detail placement

Explain pillar-shaped query initialization, projection into camera frames, and the constant-velocity approximation. Pillar queries do not imply LiDAR input. Place online versus future-frame availability and single versus dual branch beside their reported results; they change what the system can be used for. Routine widths belong in setup.

## Interpretation

Do not combine the highest offline future-frame score with the fast online configuration in one headline. Query-conditioned receptive-field patterns suggest adaptive behavior but do not prove a causal class-size law. Similarly, the small advantage of a mixing order is local evidence, not a universal semantic principle.
