## Writing move: Use a minimal counterexample to explain an objective

Stable Matching uses two imperfect predictions: one has better localization, the other a higher class score. The competition explains why one-to-one matching can reinforce inconsistent preferences. Positional supervision and a modified matching cost are then introduced as responses to that example.

## Experimental logic

Cross-layer assignment changes connect the method to stability. Loss variants test the specific claim that adding classification probability to the target can hurt in this setting. Separate rows for NMS, the loss, matching cost, and memory fusion keep the final system improvement from being attributed to one change.

## Detail placement

Explain why the loss cannot simply be reused as the matching cost: low confidence and low overlap could then look artificially favorable. The supplementary memory-fusion discussion gives a distinct early-training rationale involving pretrained backbone features and a randomly initialized encoder.

## Interpretation

The two-prediction example is an explanatory model, not a proof of global optimization uniqueness. Main experiments use NMS by default and expose its contribution; do not call every reported variant NMS-free. Distinguish the position-only classification target from the full matching cost, which still uses classification information.
