## Writing move: Answer the objections to a simpler formulation

FCOS presents per-location box prediction, then addresses two reasons a reader might expect it to fail: missed objects and ambiguous targets in overlapping boxes. Feature-pyramid assignment and center-ness make the simpler formulation practical. The introduction and method follow the same questions.

## Experimental logic

Best possible recall and ambiguous-location rates examine the objections directly. Predicting center-ness is compared with deriving it from the regressed box; only the learned branch helps in that comparison. A matched-settings baseline precedes the larger collection of improvements.

## Detail placement

Keep the point-to-boundary target and feature-level assignment rule explicit. Anchor-free removes a particular parameterization, not all scales, thresholds, or design choices. Mark later recipe improvements separately from the central formulation.

## Interpretation

Best possible recall is an assignment-based upper bound, not achieved detection recall. The overlap analysis supports adequacy in the tested setting rather than a proof that ambiguity never matters. FCOS still uses NMS.
