## Writing move: Make the stages of a label-efficiency story visible

SimCLRv2 builds around a productive surprise: large pretrained models can help most when labels are scarce. Its three-stage story is easy to follow: learn general features without labels, adapt them with a small labeled set, and transfer task-specific predictions to a compact student using unlabeled images. Compression becomes the practical consequence of scaling.

## Experimental logic

Model-size and projection-head studies explain how the teacher improves. Distillation comparisons distinguish the benefit of unlabeled teacher predictions from training with the labeled subset alone. The appendix examines parameter efficiency, the relationship between linear evaluation and fine-tuning, and a smaller dataset, giving each experiment a different explanatory job.

## Detail placement

State the unlabeled pool, labeled fraction, teacher size, and final student size near the headline. Explain which projection layers are retained for adaptation. Put expanded architectural grids and temperature sweeps in the appendix, while preserving their main conclusions.

## Interpretation

One percent labels does not mean one percent of the images, and a compact student does not imply inexpensive training of the complete pipeline. The appendix notes a possible ceiling effect in relative scaling gains. Label efficiency, training cost, and inference size are separate advantages to describe precisely.
