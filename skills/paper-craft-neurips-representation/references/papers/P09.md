## Writing move: Turn domain differences into the reason for the method

GraphCL makes heterogeneous graph semantics the central obstacle to transferring image-style augmentation. Each transformation is paired with a prior about what may change without changing the task. This gives readers a reason to care about augmentation selection beyond the existence of a contrastive pipeline.

## Experimental logic

The augmentation-pair matrix comes before the final benchmark comparisons. It reveals that edge perturbation can help social graphs but hurt a molecular dataset. Strength and degree-based masking studies investigate the proposed explanation. Training with augmentation alone provides a useful comparison for the additional role of contrastive pretraining.

## Detail placement

Keep graph-level versus node-level prediction, the augmentation pool, and the readout regime in the main account. Explain density and chemical-bond examples in ordinary language. Put random-walk pseudocode and full parameter grids in supporting material unless sampling is the new contribution.

## Interpretation

Transfer and attack results vary by dataset and architecture. The supplementary node experiments also show settings where pretraining hurts. A fixed default augmentation strength does not mean that no model selection occurred. The useful lesson is how to choose task-compatible invariances, not that every perturbation preserves graph meaning.
