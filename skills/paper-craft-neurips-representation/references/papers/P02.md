## Writing move: Use a surprising observation to motivate a simple mechanism

BYOL makes its central question immediately understandable: can a network learn useful features by predicting another network without negative examples? Learning from a fixed random target provides an early observation that motivates a moving target. The online network, predictor, and target network are then explained through their roles rather than introduced as an unexplained diagram.

## Experimental logic

Linear evaluation establishes representation quality; transfer asks whether the features remain useful elsewhere. Component changes investigate why ordinary training avoids collapse. Supplementary Section J is particularly instructive: a sufficiently fast predictor can work without the moving-average target, refining the explanation rather than invalidating the main result.

## Detail placement

Keep target updates, stopped gradients, the predictor, and the feature used at evaluation in the method. When discussing small batches, state that the main batch study accumulates gradients to preserve the effective update batch. Put detailed downstream schedules and complete predictor sweeps in supporting material.

## Interpretation

The demonstrated claim is learning useful representations without negatives. The explanation involving predictor quality and slowly changing targets remains a proposed mechanism, not a proof of global collapse avoidance. Different evaluation recipes and longer training should remain distinguishable from component effects.
