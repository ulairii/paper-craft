## Writing move: Show the ideal comparison before deriving a practical correction

Debiased Contrastive Learning motivates a loss change with an oracle comparison: random negatives can include examples from the anchor class, and truly different-class negatives perform better. A mixture-distribution argument then turns this observation into a correction computable from positives and unlabeled data. The compact pseudocode makes the practical consequence of the derivation visible.

## Experimental logic

The one-positive setting uses the same batch as the baseline, making it the cleanest demonstration of the correction. Additional positives test a different question about estimation quality and require additional computation. Vision, sentence, and control experiments examine portability, with task-dependent gains rather than one configuration winning everywhere.

## Detail placement

Keep the class-prior parameter, surrogate positive construction, and numerical fallback in the method when they affect the objective actually used. The ImageNet experiment uses 100 classes. Put extended concentration proofs and routine training schedules in supporting material.

## Interpretation

The correction estimates aggregate contamination; it does not identify every false negative. Theory assumes a particular latent-class and positive-sampling model, while practice uses augmentations or nearby sentences. The appendix changes the fallback for some applications. Explain that adaptation as part of using the principle across domains.
