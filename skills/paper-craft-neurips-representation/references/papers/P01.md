# P01: Learning Representations by Maximizing Mutual Information Across Views

Bachman, Philip; Hjelm, R Devon; Buchwalter, William. **NeurIPS 2019**. [Official paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/ddf354219aac374f1d40b7e760ee5bb7-Abstract.html) · [Full text](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/ddf354219aac374f1d40b7e760ee5bb7-Paper.pdf).

Source locations: Sections 1–4; Figures 1–2; Tables 1–3.

## Writing move: Give each part of a stronger recipe an explicit job

AMDIM starts from a useful learning signal: different views of the same image share information that survives local changes. It presents three concrete extensions to DIM: prediction across augmented views, multiple feature scales, and a stronger encoder. This makes a recipe contribution legible. The reader can see what is inherited, what changes, and why the changes belong together.

## Experimental logic

The main classification results establish useful features, while Table 3 asks which parts of the recipe matter. Cropping and color augmentation have substantial effects; the headline improvement should not be attributed to the multiscale objective alone. The small, short-training ablation and the larger final model have different roles. Retrieval examples then illustrate what the features retain, including background-sensitive failures.

## Detail placement

Explain how receptive fields support local prediction and identify which features the downstream classifier reads. Put full layer inventories and routine optimization settings after that explanation. Treat the mixture extension as a separate possibility, so it does not obscure the demonstrated recipe.

## Interpretation

A contrastive information objective does not by itself establish semantic understanding. The mixture examples suggest segmentation-like behavior but do not establish competitive dense prediction. A strong account can acknowledge the role of augmentation and model capacity while retaining a clear contribution.
