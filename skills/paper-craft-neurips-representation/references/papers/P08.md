## Writing move: Let each finding generate the next research question

The transfer-learning analysis develops through connected questions: does transfer depend only on reusable visual features, what remains after spatial disruption, how do resulting models differ, and can earlier checkpoints suffice? Each analysis motivates the next. The contribution is an explanation assembled from complementary observations rather than a new algorithm forced into the paper.

## Experimental logic

Block shuffling probes the role of spatial structure; mistakes and CKA compare model behavior; parameter interpolation examines barriers between solutions. Repeating the random-initialization experiment with identical starting weights addresses an obvious alternative explanation. Early-checkpoint experiments separate optimization progress from final target performance.

## Detail placement

Define pretrained, random, and adapted models once. Explain what block shuffling changes, including the altered augmentation setting, and what the optimization-speed proxy measures. Keep the Fixup ResNet setup and custom data subsets visible; place full interpolation galleries and module plots in the appendix.

## Interpretation

Benefits that survive shuffling suggest contributions beyond ordinary spatial feature reuse, but do not uniquely identify every mechanism. Linear-path evidence is not a characterization of the entire loss landscape. Results on the selected visual tasks do not establish a universal account of language transfer or clinical deployment.
