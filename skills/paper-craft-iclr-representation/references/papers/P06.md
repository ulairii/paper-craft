# P06: Understanding Dimensional Collapse in Contrastive Self-supervised Learning

Li Jing; Pascal Vincent; Yann LeCun; Yuandong Tian. **ICLR 2022**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=YevsQ05DEN7) · [Full text read: arXiv:2110.09348v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.09348v3).

Source locations: Sections 3–6; Figures 2–3 and 6–10; Tables 1–2; Appendices B–F.

## Writing move: Build a discovery paper around a precisely defined surprise

The surprise is not that contrastive training can fail, but that preventing constant features does not ensure use of the full embedding space. A measured spectrum establishes the phenomenon before simplified dynamics explain two possible mechanisms. DirectCLR then serves as a design consequence of the analysis, rather than as a replacement for every state-of-the-art method.

## Experimental logic

Controlled linear models separate strong augmentation from multilayer implicit regularization. The projector ablations test the proposed importance of rank and orientation. Appendix F compares a fixed subvector with random feature dropping and probes both the selected subvector and the full representation, connecting the design to useful information outside directly supervised coordinates.

## Detail placement

Define representation and projection embedding separately; their spectra answer different questions. Keep the linear-model assumptions and the actual comparison to a linear projector near the main claim. Delay proofs and tuning curves, but explain how the theory motivates the experiment.

## Interpretation

DirectCLR exceeds the linear-projector baseline in Table 1 but remains below its nonlinear-projector baseline. The authors explicitly acknowledge that the last nonlinear backbone block still serves a related function. The theory does not fully explain arbitrary nonlinear networks or establish that all dimensional reduction is harmful.
