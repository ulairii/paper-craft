# P01: Learning deep representations by mutual information estimation and maximization

R Devon Hjelm; Alex Fedorov; Samuel Lavoie-Marchildon; Karan Grewal; Phil Bachman; Adam Trischler; Yoshua Bengio. **ICLR 2019**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=Bklr3j0cKX) · [Full text read: arXiv:1808.06670v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06670v5).

Source locations: Sections 1–4; Figures 1–3; Tables 1–5; Appendices A.3–A.6.

## Writing move: Make useful information the question, not maximum information

DIM makes a distinction that gives the entire paper a purpose: retaining more information about an image need not produce better features for classification. Local–global agreement encourages information shared across patches. The method explanation assigns different jobs to local information, global information, and prior matching before introducing their estimators. Credit the information-estimation machinery, then identify the choice of structure as the contribution.

## Experimental logic

The classification, reconstruction, and dependence measurements answer different questions. Table 4 makes their disagreement informative: high estimated information does not necessarily accompany the best classifier. The comparison with CPC also changes the image-processing architecture, showing why a recipe can affect an apparent objective comparison.

## Detail placement

Keep the local/global distinction, the downstream feature layer, and the compared architecture in the main account. Put estimator alternatives, negative-count sweeps, and larger coefficient grids in supporting material unless they carry the proposed insight.

## Interpretation

The local objective is useful in the reported classification settings; the appendix also gives a reason to retain global information for fine-grained attributes. A Jensen–Shannon training objective is not a direct numerical measurement of mutual information. Low coordinate dependence does not establish causal disentanglement.
