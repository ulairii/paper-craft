## Writing move: Turn an intuitive rule into a controlled tradeoff

The opening question is concrete: which negatives are informative? The argument immediately adds the difficulty that makes it a research problem: nearby examples may be false negatives when labels are unavailable. Hardness and approximate false-negative correction become two distinct design requirements. The implementation follows from those requirements through reweighting existing batch similarities.

## Experimental logic

Figure 4 combines a hardness sweep, an informative label-assisted comparison, and a two-by-two component study. Together they explain why increasing hardness can help and then hurt. Figure 5 interprets overlapping positive/negative similarity distributions instead of assuming that every positive pair must become closer.

## Detail placement

Keep the hardness parameter, unavailable true labels, and approximate correction in the method story. State when the diagnostic uses labels. Move the importance-sampling derivation and modality-specific clipping to supporting sections, while retaining their effect on applicability.

## Interpretation

The limiting optimal-embedding result has a different scope from finite neural-network training. Benefits vary across datasets; the sentence experiments disable the correction term because of optimization issues. Reusing batch scores avoids additional encoder passes, but that is more precise than claiming literally no computation.
