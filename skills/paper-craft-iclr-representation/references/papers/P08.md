## Writing move: Use a shared formulation to make a combination feel necessary

iBOT connects masked prediction and self-distillation through a common teacher–student formulation. The teacher becomes an online tokenizer; cross-view class-token learning supplies a semantic signal, while same-view masked patch prediction models local content. Explaining these separate roles makes the combination more persuasive than listing two existing losses.

## Experimental logic

Table 9 compares the joint design with removed class supervision, an independent tokenizer, and BEiT plus DINO. Frozen patch classification and linear segmentation test the local-feature claim more directly than image-level accuracy alone. Appendix B separates multi-crop settings; Table 19 adds measured time and memory to the quality story.

## Detail placement

Keep the two token roles, online update, shared head, and evaluation protocol visible. Attach model size, ImageNet-22K pretraining, and resolution to the strongest headline scores. Put detailed temperature, masking, and crop sweeps in supporting material.

## Interpretation

The online tokenizer does not win every fine-tuning comparison, and retrieval does not improve consistently over DINO. Selected patch patterns illustrate an interpretation; quantitative local probes provide stronger support. Effective epochs approximate image-area exposure and do not equal wall-clock cost. MIM adds memory and time per training run.
