## Writing move: Organize multiple losses around sources of usable supervision

DeCLIP gives three auxiliary objectives a common purpose: use more of the supervision already available in image–text data. Its within-modality, cross-view, and neighboring-pair structure makes a many-loss system readable. The motivation is data use, so the opening evidence varies dataset size instead of relying only on one final model.

## Experimental logic

Matched-data CLIP reimplementations help distinguish the objective from data collection. The component ladder shows incremental gains in one order; it does not establish independent contributions or all interactions. Longer-training and larger-batch baselines in Appendix F address the extra work from multiple views.

## Detail placement

Name the reused image self-supervision and language modeling methods, then explain which additional pairs each loss contributes. Keep training-data identity and the resource being saved near efficiency claims. Move queue dimensions, prompt lists, and optimizer settings out of the central argument.

## Interpretation

The headline comparison uses fewer distinct pairs, not necessarily the same data distribution or lower cost per step. The method requires additional compute and memory. On the cost study, quote actual compared budgets rather than calling unequal GPU-hour runs equal. Neighbor examples suggest useful matches without proving they are always correct.
