# P07: BEiT: BERT Pre-Training of Image Transformers

Hangbo Bao; Li Dong; Songhao Piao; Furu Wei. **ICLR 2022**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=p-BhZSz59o4) · [Full text read: arXiv:2106.08254v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08254v2).

Source locations: Sections 1–3; Figure 1; Tables 1, 3–4; Appendices A–E.

## Writing move: Make the input–target distinction carry the method explanation

BEiT uses the BERT analogy to expose a specific missing ingredient: image patches lack a ready-made discrete vocabulary. The method preserves raw patches as input but predicts discrete tokenizer outputs at masked locations. This distinction makes the adaptation intelligible and identifies the prediction target, rather than the existence of a Transformer, as the design choice.

## Experimental logic

Table 4 changes target type, masking structure, and prediction locations across classification and segmentation. The larger segmentation changes explain why a small classification difference need not make a component unimportant. Appendix C replaces the external tokenizer with one trained on ImageNet, addressing a natural alternative explanation.

## Detail placement

State the tokenizer source, masking target, and full fine-tuning protocol in the main narrative. Separate self-supervised pretraining from intermediate supervised fine-tuning. Architecture-enhanced large-scale variants need their own labels. Detailed schedules and the VAE derivation can support the account.

## Interpretation

The weak linear probes in Appendix D make full fine-tuning an essential qualifier. The pixel comparison concerns the tested reconstruction recipe, not all pixel-based pretraining. Faster fine-tuning does not include the cost of pretraining. Attention examples illustrate spatial structure without establishing a general segmentation guarantee.
