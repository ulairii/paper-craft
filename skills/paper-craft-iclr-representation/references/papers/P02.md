# P02: Deep Graph Infomax

Petar Veličković; William Fedus; William L. Hamilton; Pietro Liò; Yoshua Bengio; R Devon Hjelm. **ICLR 2019**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rklz9iAcKQ) · [Full text read: arXiv:1809.10341v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10341v2).

Source locations: Sections 1, 3–5; Table 2; Figures 1 and 3; Appendices B–C.

## Writing move: Separate the objective contribution from the encoder contribution

DGI motivates a new objective by asking what random-walk proximity adds when graph convolutions already favor nearby nodes. It then adapts DIM explicitly to node patches and a graph summary. This is a useful way to sell an adaptation: name the inherited principle, explain the domain-specific obstacle, and show what the new objective makes possible.

## Experimental logic

Table 2 lists the information available to each method and includes a randomly initialized graph encoder. That baseline is especially revealing on inductive tasks: much of the advantage over earlier methods is already present before training, while DGI adds a further gain. The appendix removes selected embedding dimensions and varies corruption to investigate what the objective learns.

## Detail placement

Retain feature/adjacency/label access, transductive versus inductive evaluation, and the frozen logistic readout in the main text. Describe corruption by its learning role before listing its implementation. Extended dimensional-removal and corruption plots can support the main result.

## Interpretation

Beating selected supervised architectures does not mean beating all supervised graph learning. The paper states that the PPI gap remains large. The finite, deterministic theoretical setting motivates the objective; it does not prove that an actual average readout recovers every useful graph property.
