# P04: Large-Scale Representation Learning on Graphs via Bootstrapping

Shantanu Thakoor; Corentin Tallec; Mohammad Gheshlaghi Azar; Mehdi Azabou; Eva L Dyer; Remi Munos; Petar Veličković; Michal Valko. **ICLR 2022**. [Official paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0UXT6PpRpW) · [Full text read: arXiv:2102.06514v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.06514v3).

Source locations: Sections 3–4; Tables 1, 3–6; Figures 4–5; Appendices A–B and G–J.

## Writing move: Let successive scale regimes organize the evidence

BGRL sells a practical consequence of removing negative comparisons: useful graph learning at larger scales. Its experiments progress from graphs where the full contrastive baseline fits, to graphs requiring approximation, to a very large semi-supervised setting. Each stage answers a new question rather than repeating the same leaderboard claim.

## Experimental logic

Measured memory accompanies the complexity argument. Varying the number of sampled negatives exposes the baseline quality–memory tradeoff. At the largest scale, the unlabeled-data ratio tests whether the objective can use additional information. The simpler-encoder study in Appendix H helps separate the learning objective from a stronger architecture.

## Detail placement

The switch from frozen evaluation to joint supervised/self-supervised training belongs beside the large-scale claim. So does the distinction between validation ablations and the competition ensemble. Encoder widths, sampling constants, and further stability plots belong in supporting material.

## Interpretation

Appendix J reports weak frozen-feature results on MAG240M, where GRACE does better. The large-scale success should therefore be described as semi-supervised. Linear complexity assumes suitable encoders and is contrasted with the specified all-pairs method, not every possible contrastive implementation. Contest rankings cannot isolate the objective.
